\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Every autonomous vehicle that must operate unattended --- a robotaxi that never sees a
technician, a delivery drone flying forty sorties a day, an autonomous underwater vehicle six
months into a deployment, a spacecraft that has not been touched since integration --- runs into
the same wall. Its battery must be pushed hard enough to be useful and held back enough to be
safe, and the state that decides where that line falls is not measurable in the field. The
standard answers are to identify the pack online, which requires excitation nobody can afford,
or to solve a constrained optimisation every control step, whose iteration count a safety task
slot cannot be sized around.

This paper develops a third answer and takes it across four physical media. Building on a solver-free safety filter for battery fast charging \cite{krishnan2026safe},
we prove that when a system's constraints are monotone in a scalar input and one
always-available input is safe,
the admissible input set collapses to a single interval whose edges are found by bisection ---
in a \emph{fixed} number of one-step model evaluations, with no optimiser, no convergence test,
and no identification of the plant. The original form of this result assumes the safe input is
zero, which is true of a cell being charged and false of essentially every vehicle doing its
job: cut the current to a rotorcraft and it falls; cut it to a submersible and it loses the
computer navigating it; cut it to a spacecraft in eclipse and the payload browns out.

We show that this apparent failure is a misdiagnosis. Zero \emph{is} still safe for every
temperature and voltage constraint on a discharging vehicle; what zero cannot do is serve the
load, and that is a constraint of a different kind --- a floor rather than a cap --- for which
the original theorem has no vocabulary. \textbf{Anchored Collapse} adds the floor family and a
second bisection, raising the worst-case cost from \evalCharge{} to \evalDischarge{} model
evaluations, both fixed. It reduces to the published filter \cite{krishnan2026safe} bit-for-bit on
\reduceStates{} states.

Across \vPlatforms{} vehicle platforms in four media --- ground, air, water and vacuum --- and
\vExperiments{} experiments totalling \vTotalEpisodes{} simulated episodes, no plant constraint
was breached while the certificate reported feasible. The admissible set was a single interval
in all \scanTotal{} dense scans. Worst-case latency across every platform was \worstLatency{}
$\mu$s, or \slotFraction\,\% of a 10\,ms ISO~26262 task slot.

The theorem and the bisection's soundness are \textbf{machine-checked in Lean~4}: seven
theorems, no \texttt{sorry}, and no dependency on any axiom. And the two assumptions the method
rests on are tested against \nCells{} cells measured by another laboratory: real resistance
growth stays inside the assumed bound in \nInsideEolFrac\,\% of \nEolMeas{} measurements over
the life the certificate claims, and heat generation is confirmed monotone in current across
\nDisCycles{} measured cycles ($\rho = \nDisRho$), model-free.

Four results contradicted the hypotheses they were written to test, and are reported as
findings rather than repaired: the certificate \emph{refuses} the calibrated cell for a
rotorcraft before takeoff above \aRefuseC{}C of hover draw; in a sealed pressure hull the
binding constraint is plating at every water temperature, which is the one constraint the
theorem enforces but does not certify; in deep space a \emph{smaller} radiator delivers more
charge, because the pack is cold-limited rather than heat-limited; and under an ice shelf closure leaves only \uIceClosureMed{} minutes before an actual breach
against a \uIceTransit{}-minute transit to safety, which makes closure the wrong trigger and
converts a negative result into a quantitative design rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The limiting resource for long-duration autonomy is not perception, planning, or control. It is
\emph{energy under certainty}: how hard a vehicle may push its battery when nobody is available
to inspect it, and how confident anyone can be in the answer.

The gap between what a lithium-ion pack can physically deliver and what a battery management
system will actually permit is large, and it is not there by accident. Fast charging drives
three coupled failure modes --- thermal runaway, over-potential, and lithium plating on the
anode --- whose onset depends on internal states that a production sensor suite does not
measure. Series resistance roughly doubles over a pack's service life, plating risk climbs as
temperature falls, and the safe current at end of life in cold weather is a fraction of the
safe current when new and warm. A manufacturer facing that uncertainty does the only defensible
thing and de-rates for the worst case, permanently.

For a privately owned car this is a mild inefficiency. For an autonomous fleet it is the
economics of the whole enterprise. A robotaxi that charges twelve times a day accumulates a
private car's decade of fast-charge stress in \gWeeks{} weeks (\S\ref{sec:ground}); the
de-rate it inherits was designed for a vehicle with a fundamentally different duty cycle. For a
spacecraft it is worse still, because the de-rate is chosen before launch and can never be
revisited.

\subsection{Two standard answers, and why neither reaches the vehicle}

The first is \emph{identification}: estimate the pack's internal state online and de-rate against
the estimate. Estimating resistance and capacity well requires excitation --- current profiles
rich enough to be informative --- which an operational vehicle is not free to inject. Worse, the
guarantee becomes conditional on the estimator having converged, which is a claim about a
transient nobody audits.

The second is \emph{constrained optimisation}: solve a small model-predictive control problem
each step and let the solver enforce the constraints. This is the standard modern answer, and a
good one, with a specific defect --- a quadratic program's iteration count depends on its data.
A safety-critical task slot is sized for the worst case, and if the worst case is unbounded the
slot cannot be sized at all. In practice this is handled with iteration caps and fallback logic:
by having a plan for when the guarantee does not hold.

\subsection{The result}

We take a third path, whose value is that it needs neither. The observation is geometric rather
than numerical:

\begin{quote}
If every constraint is monotone in a scalar input, and one input is known to be safe, then the
set of admissible inputs is not a complicated region to be searched. It is a single interval,
and its edges can be located by bisection in a fixed number of one-step model evaluations.
\end{quote}

Bisection needs no optimiser, no convergence test, and --- critically --- no identification of
the plant, because a monotone \emph{bound} on the uncertain parameter suffices where a point
estimate would be required. A datasheet end-of-life resistance figure is such a bound and is
known before the vehicle ships.

The contribution of this paper is to establish that this result survives contact with real
vehicles, which required repairing it first. Section~\ref{sec:theory} shows that the failure
observed when the filter is placed on a hovering rotorcraft is not the failure it appears to be,
and develops \textbf{Anchored Collapse}, which covers discharge as well as charge. Sections
\ref{sec:ground}--\ref{sec:space} then take it across four media --- ground, air, water, vacuum
--- across \vPlatforms{} platforms and \vExperiments{} experiments.

\paragraph{Contributions.} \textbf{(i)} \emph{Anchored Collapse} (\S\ref{sec:anchored}), a
generalisation covering constraints requiring the input to be \emph{large} enough --- what every
vehicle's irreducible load imposes and what the original theorem cannot express --- at a cost of
\evalDischarge{} fixed evaluations rather than \evalCharge{}. \textbf{(ii)} A two-sided
certificate and a \emph{reserve} (\S\ref{sec:reserve}): the gap between the edges is how much
room the vehicle has left, which the one-sided version cannot report and which yields an abort
rule for vehicles that cannot afford to find their limit empirically. \textbf{(iii)} A
certificate that refuses \emph{designs}, not just commands (\S\ref{sec:refusal}).
\textbf{(iv)} Application across four media (\S\ref{sec:ground}--\S\ref{sec:space}), including
where the result does \emph{not} hold and what that costs.

\section{The autonomy energy bottleneck}\label{sec:bottleneck}

Why a battery certificate is a bottleneck for autonomy rather than a component-level concern is
worth being concrete about, because the four application sections that follow are chosen to span
the ways it binds.

\paragraph{Ground fleets are duty-cycle limited.} A robotaxi's economics are set by
utilisation, and utilisation is set by how long it spends attached to a charger. The
constraint is not the charger --- \S\ref{sec:ground} shows a 350\,kW station is already past
what the pack will accept --- but how aggressively the pack may be driven at the temperature it
happens to be, at the age it happens to be, with no technician in the loop. That is precisely
the quantity a certificate computes.

\paragraph{Aerial autonomy is margin limited.} A rotorcraft in hover draws a continuous
1--3C from its pack. There is no coasting, no regeneration, and no graceful degradation: an
aircraft that runs out of admissible current does not slow down, it descends. Every kilogram of
battery carried to buy margin is a kilogram not carried as payload, so the margin is contested
directly against the mission.

\paragraph{Underwater autonomy is abort limited.} An AUV under an ice shelf has no
abort-to-surface. The vehicle must decide to turn back while turning back is still possible,
using only what it can compute onboard, hours or days from any human. A certificate that is
correct but late is indistinguishable from one that is wrong.

\paragraph{Space autonomy is calibration limited.} A spacecraft's battery model is fixed at
integration and cannot be updated in any meaningful sense afterwards --- the downlink budget is
not for battery parameters, and the round-trip light time to the outer planets is hours. Over a
five-year mission the pack ages, takes radiation dose, and cycles tens of thousands of times
against a parameter set that has not changed since before launch. \textbf{The property that
makes a solver-free certificate merely convenient on a car is the property that makes it
admissible at all on a spacecraft}: it requires no identification, so there is nothing to
recalibrate.

These four are not a list of markets. They are four different ways for the same underlying
guarantee to be the binding constraint, and a method that addresses one by exploiting its
specifics would not transfer. What follows is deliberately one method, unmodified, in all four.

\subsection{Related work}

The framework is control invariance \cite{blanchini2008settheoretic}: a set is
control-invariant if from every state in it some input keeps the next state inside. Control
barrier functions \cite{ames2019cbf} and predictive safety filters \cite{wabersich2021psf}
enforce this by solving an optimisation each step; reference governors
\cite{gilbert2002governor} and shielding \cite{alshiekh2018shielding} are related. Ours is a
predictive safety filter whose optimisation has been replaced by a bisection, which is possible
only because of monotonicity \cite{angeli2003monotone,coogan2015mixed} --- order-preserving
structure that lets worst-case reachability be computed from extreme points rather than by
searching an interior. Our use is narrower, monotonicity in a scalar \emph{input} rather than in
the state, and it is what turns a search into two bisections.

The reference battery model is Doyle--Fuller--Newman \cite{doyle1993,newman2004} with Bernardi
thermal coupling \cite{bernardi1985}, in PyBaMM \cite{sulzer2021pybamm} with the Chen
\cite{chen2020} and O'Kane \cite{okane2022} parameter sets; plating is the dominant fast-charge
degradation mode \cite{yang2017plating}. Online estimation of resistance and capacity is mature
\cite{plett2015,moura2017} --- and is exactly what this method deliberately does not require.
Reinforcement learning has been applied to fast charging \cite{wei2022ddpg,attia2020}, and
constrained RL offers expectation-level guarantees \cite{achiam2017cpo,altman1999constrained},
which are the wrong shape for a constraint that must hold on every step of every episode. Our
position is that the policy may be learned and the safety must not be.

\section{The cell, and which of its constraints are monotone}\label{sec:cell}

The result of \S\ref{sec:theory} is a statement about constraints, so it is worth writing the
constraints down. Everything in this paper rests on a reduced-order electro-thermal model of a
lithium-ion cell, calibrated against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman simulation in PyBaMM and reported
in \cite{krishnan2026safe} at a held-out voltage RMSE of 20.3\,mV and temperature RMSE of
1.32\,\degC{} across ambients from 0 to 25\,\degC{}. The model is prior work; what matters here
is its \emph{shape}, because that is what the theorem consumes.

\paragraph{State and outputs.} The state is $x = (\mathrm{soc}, T, V_1)$ --- state of charge,
cell temperature, and the overpotential stored in a single RC branch --- with a slowly varying
ageing pair (capacity loss, resistance growth) carried alongside. For an applied current $I$
(positive charging) over a step $\Delta t$, the model returns terminal voltage, the next
temperature, and a plating-potential proxy:
\begin{align}
V &= \mathrm{OCV}(\mathrm{soc}) \;+\; \underbrace{I\,R_\Omega(\mathrm{soc},T)}_{\text{ohmic}}
   \;+\; \underbrace{A\big(1+c_T (T-25)\big)\,\mathrm{asinh}\!\big(I/2i_0(\mathrm{soc})\big)}_{\text{activation}}
   \;+\; V_1, \label{eq:V}\\[2pt]
C_{\mathrm{th}}\,\dot T &= \underbrace{I\,\eta_\Omega + I\,\eta_{\mathrm{act}}
   + V_1^2/R_1}_{\text{irreversible}} \;+\; \underbrace{I\,(T+273.15)\,\partial U/\partial T}_{\text{reversible}}
   \;-\; q_{\mathrm{out}}(T), \label{eq:T}\\[2pt]
\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} &= p_0(\mathrm{soc}) \;-\; p_1(\mathrm{soc},T)\,I, \qquad p_1 > 0.
\label{eq:phi}
\end{align}
$R_\Omega$ carries an Arrhenius temperature dependence, so resistance rises as the cell cools;
$q_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the cooling law, and it is the \emph{only} thing that changes between the
four media of \S\ref{sec:ground}--\S\ref{sec:space}.

\paragraph{Why each constraint is monotone in current.} This is the property the theorem needs,
and each one has a physical reason rather than an empirical one.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Voltage} \eqref{eq:V}. Both overpotential terms are odd and increasing in $I$
($\mathrm{asinh}$ is strictly increasing), and $\mathrm{OCV}$ does not depend on $I$. So $V$
increases in charge current and decreases in discharge current: a cap on charge, a cap on
discharge as well, because the constraint there is $V \ge V_{\min}$ on a signal that falls.
\item \textbf{Temperature} \eqref{eq:T}. Every generation term is non-decreasing in $|I|$ ---
$I\eta_\Omega = I^2 R_\Omega \ge 0$ regardless of sign --- and $q_{\mathrm{out}}$ does not
depend on $I$ at all. So the next temperature is non-decreasing in current, whatever the cooling
law, which is why replacing Newtonian convection with Stefan--Boltzmann radiation
(\S\ref{sec:space}) leaves the certificate's structure untouched.
\item \textbf{Plating} \eqref{eq:phi} is \emph{affine} and strictly decreasing in $I$. Plating
occurs when $\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} \le 0$.
\item \textbf{Delivered power} $P = S\,V(I)\,I$ is increasing in $I$ below the maximum-power
point --- the one condition of Remark~\ref{rem:mpp}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why plating is enforced and not certified.} This distinction recurs throughout the
paper and it is a consequence of \eqref{eq:phi} rather than a modelling choice. Forward
invariance requires an always-available input that returns the state to the safe set. At $I=0$,
$\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} = p_0(\mathrm{soc})$, and $p_0$ falls with state of charge: above roughly
0.82\,SOC it is already below the required margin. \textbf{There is no current --- not even zero
--- that clears the plating margin at high state of charge}, so no one-step condition can render
it invariant, and no honest certificate can claim it.

The certified set is therefore $\mathcal{S} = \{T \le T_{\max},\; V \le V_{\max}\}$, and plating
is \emph{enforced} by the same bisection plus a temperature-dependent current cap, and monitored.
It rides the same monotone structure, so it costs nothing extra to enforce; it simply cannot be
promised. \S\ref{sec:water} is where that distinction stops being bookkeeping and becomes the
binding constraint of an entire vehicle class.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

Let a system have state $x$, scalar input $u \in [u_{\min}, u_{\max}]$, one-step map
$x^+ = f(x,u,w)$, and a finite family of constraints $c_j(x,u)\le 0$ defining a safe set
$\mathcal{S}$. Write $\mathcal{U}(x) = \{u : c_j(x,u)\le 0\ \forall j\}$ for the admissible input
set.

\subsection{Null-Input Collapse}

\begin{proposition}[Null-Input Collapse]\label{prop:nic}
Suppose \textbf{(A1)} $u = 0$ is safe --- from every $x \in \mathcal{S}$ the one-step map at
$u=0$ returns to $\mathcal{S}$ --- and \textbf{(A2)} each $c_j$ is non-decreasing in $u$. Then
$\mathcal{U}(x) = [0, u^\ast(x)]$ is a single interval anchored at zero, $\mathcal{S}$ is
forward-invariant under any policy filtered through it, and $u^\ast$ is computed to precision
$\varepsilon$ in $\lceil \log_2 (u_{\max}/\varepsilon)\rceil + 2$ one-step evaluations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By (A2) each $\{u : c_j \le 0\}$ is a down-set; a finite intersection of down-sets in
$\mathbb{R}$ is a down-set, hence an interval $[u_{\min}, u^\ast]$, which by (A1) contains $0$.
Bisection on a monotone predicate converges linearly with an iteration count depending only on
the requested precision, not on the data. Forward invariance follows by induction: each filtered
step lands in $\mathcal{S}$ and $u=0$ remains available.
\end{proof}

Two things are worth isolating. First, no step requires knowing the plant's uncertain
parameters --- only evaluating $c_j$ under a \emph{bound} on them, and a conservative bound
yields a conservative interval. Second, the cost is $O(\log 1/\varepsilon)$ \emph{deterministic}
evaluations, which is what a task slot can be sized around and a solver's iteration count is
not.

\subsection{Where it breaks, and why that reading is wrong}\label{sec:anchored}

Placed on a quadrotor holding altitude, a filter built on Proposition~\ref{prop:nic}
\cite{krishnan2026safe} passes
every one-step check for a mean of 22.3 consecutive steps and then violates in 100\,\% of
episodes. The natural reading is that (A1) has failed: $u=0$ is plainly not safe for an
aircraft.

That reading is wrong, and the correction is the technical heart of this paper. Consider what
$u=0$ actually does to a discharging pack. No current flows, so there is no $I^2R$ heating and
the temperature relaxes toward ambient. Terminal voltage returns to open-circuit, so it cannot
sag below a floor. State of charge does not fall. \textbf{Every temperature and voltage
constraint is satisfied at $u=0$, on a quadrotor in flight exactly as on a cell being charged.}

What $u = 0$ cannot do is fly the aircraft. The constraint that failed is of a different kind:
a requirement that the input be \emph{large} enough. Call these \emph{floors}, as against the
\emph{caps} of Proposition~\ref{prop:nic}. Every vehicle has at least one, because every vehicle
has an irreducible load --- the power it cannot stop drawing and remain a vehicle. Hover thrust
for a rotorcraft. Hotel load for a submersible, whose computer and inertial navigation draw
their watts whether the thrusters do or not, so that even a stationary AUV cannot command zero.
Bus load for a spacecraft in eclipse. Contracted export power for a car doing vehicle-to-grid.

Charging is the special case where the irreducible load is zero --- which is exactly why the
original filter could anchor at zero without ever noticing it was making a choice.

\begin{theorem}[Anchored Collapse]\label{thm:anchored}
Partition the constraints into caps $\mathcal{C}$, each satisfied on $[u_{\min}, u_c]$, and
floors $\mathcal{F}$, each satisfied on $[u_c, u_{\max}]$. Suppose
\textbf{(A1$'$)} $u = u_{\min}$ is safe for every cap, from every $x \in \mathcal{S}$, and
\textbf{(A2)} each constraint is monotone in $u$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{U}(x) \;=\; [\,u_{\mathrm{lo}}(x),\; u_{\mathrm{hi}}(x)\,]
\;=\;\Big[\;\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} u_{c_j},\;\; \min_{j\in\mathcal{C}} u_{c_j}\;\Big],
\end{equation}
both edges are found by bisection in $O(\log 1/\varepsilon)$ one-step evaluations, and
$\mathcal{U}(x)\neq\emptyset$ exactly when $u_{\mathrm{lo}} \le u_{\mathrm{hi}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Each cap's satisfying set is a down-set and each floor's an up-set; their finite intersections
are $[u_{\min}, u_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ and $[u_{\mathrm{lo}}, u_{\max}]$, whose intersection is the
stated interval. (A1$'$) places $u_{\min}$ inside the cap family, giving the upper bisection a
known-good left endpoint; $u_{\max}$ is inside the floor family if anything is, giving the lower
bisection its right endpoint. Neither bisection needs the load current as a seed.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:nic} is the case $\mathcal{F} = \emptyset$. Note that (A1$'$) is
\emph{strictly weaker} than (A1): it asks only that zero satisfy the caps, which is the part
that survives contact with a vehicle.

\begin{remark}[The side of a constraint is data, not inference]
The plating constraint is $\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} \ge \delta$ --- a lower-bound test on a signal
that \emph{decreases} in current, so it caps current from above. Sense and side coincide only
when the constrained signal increases in $u$. Inferring side from the operator would file that
cap among the floors and let the filter command straight through it: silent, and unsafe. Plants
declare the side; an undeclared constraint raises rather than defaults.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A soundness condition that is not obvious]\label{rem:mpp}
Delivered bus power $P = S\,V(u)\,u$ with $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}u<0$ peaks at the maximum-power
point and \emph{falls} beyond it, so past that peak the set where the load is met stops being an
up-set and the lower bisection would search a non-monotone family. The voltage cap prevents it:
a pack reaches its maximum-power point near half its open-circuit voltage, far below any usable
$V_{\min}$, so $u_{\mathrm{hi}} < u_{\mathrm{MPP}}$ always and the floor search is bounded by
$u_{\mathrm{hi}}$. This ordering is checked on every discharge platform, not assumed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Machine-checked}\label{sec:formal}

Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored} and the soundness of the bisection are formalised in Lean~4 and
checked mechanically (\texttt{formal/AnchoredCollapse.lean}). Seven theorems compile with no
\texttt{sorry}, and Lean reports that each \textbf{depends on no axioms at all} --- not
classical choice, not propositional extensionality. The proofs turned out to need less than
the paper assumes: they are stated over an arbitrary type carrying a $\le$ relation, with no
order axiom used anywhere, so they hold without transitivity, antisymmetry or totality.

The property worth checking mechanically is not the collapse theorem but
\texttt{bisect\_sound}: \emph{every} state the bisection can reach has a feasible lower
endpoint. Convergence says how good the answer is; this says whether it is safe, and it holds
independent of iteration count, of the midpoint rule, and of floating-point behaviour --- the
proof never assumes the midpoint is the arithmetic mean, only that it lies between the
endpoints. A filter interrupted by a deadline, starved of its budget, or run with a sloppy
division still returns an admissible input.

\subsection{Cost}

Two probes bracket the cap family, then eighteen halvings; two more bracket the floors, then
eighteen more. Worst case \evalDischarge{} one-step model evaluations on discharge and
\evalCharge{} on charge, both fixed and both branch-bounded. Measured worst p99 latency across
all \vPlatforms{} platforms is \worstLatency{}\,$\mu$s (\worstLatencyCase{}), which is
\slotFraction\,\% of a 10\,ms ISO~26262 task slot. On the ground platforms the figure is
\groundLatency{}\,$\mu$s, or \groundSlotPct\,\%.

The property that matters is not that the number is small. It is that it does not move with the
data.

\subsection{The reserve}\label{sec:reserve}

The two-sided certificate makes available a quantity the one-sided version cannot express: the
width $u_{\mathrm{hi}} - u_{\mathrm{lo}}$ shrinks to zero as the envelope closes, so the filter
can report \emph{how close to unrecoverable} a vehicle is before it arrives. We treat this claim
adversarially in \S\ref{sec:aerial} and scope it in \S\ref{sec:cross}: it carries the most
operational weight and is the most likely to be overstated.

\section{Method and platforms}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The filter}

Given a proposed input $u_{\mathrm{req}}$, the filter returns
$\mathrm{clip}(u_{\mathrm{req}}, u_{\mathrm{lo}}, u_{\mathrm{hi}})$, or reports the interval
empty. Empty is not a violation: it is the certificate stating correctly that the irreducible
load can no longer be served inside the envelope, which is the true statement and the one a
mission planner needs.

Robustness enters through margins rather than estimation: a thermal throttle
$\delta_T = \delta_{T0} + K(s_R - 1)$ scaled by the \emph{assumed} resistance bound $s_R$, with
$K$ the smallest value certifying cells past the rated bound, and a cooling reserve $f$ that
certifies any cooling loss up to $f/(1+f)$.

The cell underneath is the reduced-order model of \cite{krishnan2026safe} in every case --- same
overpotential form, same plating proxy, same calibrated coefficients --- with two changes the
vehicles require. Current may be negative, because vehicles discharge. And the Newtonian cooling
term is a callable, because a quadrotor at 400\,m, an AUV at 2\,\degC{} and a spacecraft in
vacuum do not share a cooling law. Under the Newtonian law with positive current the vehicle
cell reproduces the published model \emph{bit for bit}, and every result downstream inherits
that.

Pack geometry, hover powers, hotel loads and orbit parameters are
\emph{class-representative} --- placing each vehicle in the right region of its class's design
space using published figures --- and are not any manufacturer's design. The purpose is that
``it works on autonomous vehicles'' is not a testable sentence, while ``it holds a 96S45P pack
under a 30-minute 350\,kW session at 35\,\degC{} ambient'' is.

\subsection{Experimental discipline}

Every experiment runs a \emph{pessimistic estimator against a drawn plant}: the filter is given a
platform at the conservative corner of the parameter envelope, and the plant it drives is drawn
from inside it. A filter tested against its own model is testing arithmetic. One property makes
that corner well defined for a two-sided certificate --- scaling the assumed resistance up
lowers the upper edge \emph{and} raises the lower edge, so the interval shrinks from both sides
under one perturbation --- and it is verified numerically, not assumed.

The experimental surface, including what would falsify each claim, was fixed in a design
register before any code ran; divergences between the register and what happened are recorded
rather than edited into agreement. Zero-failure claims are reported with Clopper--Pearson 95\,\%
one-sided upper bounds \cite{clopper1934}; continuous outcomes with bootstrap confidence
intervals; paired comparisons with Wilcoxon signed-rank and effect sizes; monotonicity claims
with permutation Spearman.

\section{Ground: robotaxis, shuttles, autonomous trucks}\label{sec:ground}

The ground domain is where the original result should hold most directly --- the anchor is zero,
the medium is a liquid loop, the physics is what the model was calibrated on. What is new is
scale and duty.

\subsection{The filter is unchanged, at pack scale}

On one cell the vehicle filter is bit-exact with the published projection: \gReduceMismatch{}
mismatches in \gReduceStates{} states, across both discretisations, fresh and aged. A
$P$-parallel pack's admissible current is exactly $P$ times one cell's, to a relative deviation
of $\gPackDev$. And on a \gTruckCells{}-cell haul-truck pack the admissible current equals the
minimum over its heterogeneous cells to $\gTruckDev$\,A --- below the $\gTruckRes$\,A resolution
of the search itself. A pack-averaged thermal limit on that many cells is a statement about a
cell that does not exist; the weakest-cell result is what makes a per-cell certificate tractable.

\subsection{Megawatt charging and the weakest cell}

The haul truck is the scale case, and it is where the per-cell architecture stops being a design
preference. A 288S115P pack is \gTruckCells{} cells with individually different resistances,
capacities and plating margins. A pack-averaged thermal limit on that many cells is a statement
about a cell that does not exist --- the average cell is not the one that fails.

The per-cell alternative is only tractable because of a structural result: the pack's admissible
current is exactly the minimum over its cells'. We verify this from $N=1$ to $N=\gTruckCells{}$
with heterogeneous ageing, and the agreement is $\gTruckDev$\,A --- below the $\gTruckRes$\,A
resolution of the search itself, which is to say exact. The consequence is that a fleet operator
does not need \gTruckCells{} filters; one filter evaluated at the weakest cell's parameters is
provably the pack's answer.

Megawatt charging makes this urgent rather than academic. The binding constraint at that power
is not the connector or the cable; it is the coldest, most-aged cell in a 600\,kWh pack, and
identifying which cell that is by measurement is not something a truck stop can do.

\subsection{A 350\,kW session}

Under the full parameter envelope, \gFastN{} sessions on a 78\,kWh robotaxi pack at 35\,\degC{}
produced \gFastViol{} violations (Clopper--Pearson 95\,\% upper bound \gFastCP\,\%), with a worst
plant temperature of \gFastWorstT\,\degC{} against a 45\,\degC{} limit, and the pessimistic
corner dominating every draw. Note which constraint binds: 350\,kW into a 96S pack is about
\gChargerA\,A, above the pack's own 3C ceiling of \gPackCeilA\,A. \textbf{The charger is not the
limiting element; the certificate is.}

Over five years of robotaxi duty --- \gRoboSessions{} fast-charge sessions with the filter given
a fixed datasheet bound on day one and told nothing afterwards --- there were \gRoboViol{}
violations (CP-95 \gRoboCP\,\%). At twelve DC fast charges a day against a private car's one a
fortnight, a factor of \gStressRatio{}, a robotaxi accumulates a private car's \emph{decade} of
fast-charge stress in \gWeeks{} weeks.

\subsection{The result this domain was not looking for}

Coupled straight to ambient air, the throttled margin puts the admissible ceiling at
$T_{\max}-\delta_T = \gCeiling\,\degC$. Above roughly that ambient there is \emph{no} admissible
fast charge at all, and the certificate delivers nothing --- correctly, because at the datasheet
end-of-life resistance bound there is none to deliver. The measured dead zone begins at
\gDeadZone\,\degC{}, the first grid node past the prediction.

Running the identical sweep with a loop holding coolant at 25\,\degC{} recovers the whole range
and buys \textbf{+\gLoopGain{} points of delivered charge} on average, with \gFastViol{}
violations in all \gAmbTrials{} sessions across both configurations. That difference is exactly
what a thermal management system purchases, quantified, and it is why production packs carry
chillers rather than radiators.

A smaller finding follows. Delivered charge is not monotone in ambient and was never going to
be: it peaks at \gPeakAmb\,\degC{}, rising along the cold limb ($\rho = \gColdRho$) where series
resistance limits and falling along the hot limb ($\rho = \gHotRho$) where the thermal margin
does. A \emph{binary} service metric --- ``will it make 60\,\% in thirty minutes'' --- jumps the
full range between adjacent grid cells, because thresholding a smooth quantity produces a
threshold. Dispatch should plan on the curve the filter already computes, not on the binary
derived from it.

\subsection{What the certificate is worth to a fleet}

These quantities translate into fleet operations directly, and the mechanism is the clearest
case in this paper of a safety result having an economic consequence.

A production pack ships with a de-rate chosen for a worst case: end of life, temperature
extreme, weakest cell. That worst case is almost never the present case. A vehicle that is warm,
mid-life and mid-charge is being held to a limit designed for a vehicle that is none of those
things. The certificate replaces the fixed de-rate with an evaluation at the \emph{current}
state under a bound that is still conservative --- nothing is given up in safety --- and the
difference is recovered continuously.

The \gLoopGain{}-point gain from active cooling above is one instance made measurable. So is the
ambient curve: a dispatcher who knows delivered charge peaks at \gPeakAmb\,\degC{} and falls
along each limb for a different physical reason can schedule charging around weather rather than
against it. And the stress-equivalence result sharpens the whole question --- a robotaxi is not
a car that drives more, it is a vehicle whose battery ages at roughly \gStressRatio{} times the
rate its de-rate was designed around. A guarantee holding for \gRoboSessions{} sessions with no
recalibration is what makes that duty cycle defensible rather than merely profitable.

\subsection{Sensing, transients, and vehicle-to-grid}

A thermistor reading low is survivable up to a breakpoint with a closed form,
$b^\ast = \delta_T/(1 - \Delta t\, hA / C_{\mathrm{th}}) = \gBiasPred\,\degC$, using the
throttled margin actually in force. The measured breakpoint is \gBiasMeas\,\degC{} on a
0.1\,\degC{} grid --- an error of \textbf{\gBiasErr\,\degC{}}, from published constants with
nothing fitted. Regenerative-braking pulses at 10\,Hz over \gRegenN{} episodes produced zero
certified violations, and a coolant-degradation sweep over \gCoolTrials{} trials violated
nothing anywhere inside the region the cooling reserve predicts.

Finally, vehicle-to-grid gives the ground domain its own instance of the generalisation, which
matters because it removes the objection that anchored collapse is a story about aircraft. At
7.4, 11 and 22\,kW of contracted export --- anchors up to \gVtwoGMaxAnchor\,A --- the anchored
filter recorded zero unsafe episodes in \gVtwoGN{} sessions each, while the null-input filter
dropped the contracted load in \textbf{\gVtwoGBrown{} of \gVtwoGN{}} at every power.

\section{Air: delivery rotorcraft and eVTOL}\label{sec:aerial}

This is the domain the generalisation exists for, and the one where the null-input filter does
not merely under-perform but loses the aircraft. On identical seeds, \aFlights{} flights each:
the anchored filter recorded \aAnchUnsafe{} unsafe-while-certified episodes; the null-input
filter starved the rotors in \textbf{\aNullBrown{} of \aFlights{}}, serving a mean of
\aNullSteps{} steps before brownout. It does not fly the aircraft for a while and then fail. It
never flies it at all.

The two-sided admissible set is genuinely an interval: \aScanStates{} flight states scanned
densely returned \aScanDisc{} disconnected sets, with every bisected edge within one grid step
of the dense scan. Across the whole flight envelope --- payload $\times$ altitude, gusts to
$\sigma=0.5$ (\aGustTrials{} flights), cold soak at 2\,000\,m, motor-out to $+100\,\%$ power
(\aMotorTrials{} flights) --- nothing was breached while the certificate reported feasible.

\subsection{The certificate refuses to fly the calibrated cell}\label{sec:refusal}

The most interesting aerial result is a refusal. With the model's own calibrated coefficients
--- an energy cell --- the anchored filter reports the interval \textbf{empty before takeoff} at
and above \aRefuseC{}C of hover draw. The admissible width collapses smoothly toward it:
5.06\,A at 0.6C, 1.28\,A at 1.0C, 0.16\,A at 1.1C, empty at \aRefuseC{}C
(Fig.~\ref{fig:domains}c).

This is not a filter failure. It is the filter working as a \emph{design tool}. The correct
engineering answer to ``may I fly a rotorcraft on an energy cell'' is no, and here that answer
arrives before the aircraft leaves the ground rather than as a thermal event in flight. A
runtime guard is not normally able to produce it, because a runtime guard is only ever asked
about the vehicle it is already installed in.

The remaining aerial experiments therefore use a power cell --- $0.40\times$ resistance,
$0.60\times$ capacity --- which is the largest single modelling liberty in this work and is
flagged wherever it applies.

\subsection{The reserve warns, and the takeoff transient sizes the pack}

Across \aLeadN{} flights, \emph{every} closure preceded its breach: \aLeadNoWarn{} breaches with
no prior warning, median lead \aLeadMed{}\,s, 5th percentile \aLeadPfive{}\,s, 95th percentile
\aLeadPninefive{}\,s. Under gusts the result holds at every variance up to $\sigma=0.5$ with
\aGustUnsafe{} unsafe episodes in \aGustTrials{} flights --- once the \emph{live} closure is
distinguished from a debounced one. Requiring three consecutive empty steps before declaring
closure costs up to \aGustDebounceHi{}\,s at the highest gust variance, and measuring lead from
the debounced signal made an earlier version of this experiment report \emph{negative} lead
times for a filter that had already closed correctly.

A full delivery sortie --- takeoff, climb, cruise, hover over the drop, return, land --- shows
the reserve tracking the mission without the filter being told a mission plan exists:
\aReserveTakeoff\,A in takeoff, \aReserveClimb\,A in climb, \aReserveCruiseOut\,A in cruise,
\aReserveHoverDrop\,A over the drop (Fig.~\ref{fig:sortie}).

\textbf{Takeoff is the binding phase, and it is what sizes the pack.} On 6S2P the certificate
refuses a $1.75\times$ takeoff transient outright; on 6S3P it allows it and \aSortieComplete\,\%
of sorties complete; on 6S4P, \textbf{\aSortieBigger\,\%}, with \aSortieUnsafe{} unsafe episodes
throughout. The frontier is actionable, which is what separates a design tool from a complaint.

Payload and density altitude close the envelope through the same physical term --- momentum
theory places both inside $T^{3/2}/\sqrt{\rho A}$ --- and the reserve falls with each:
$\rho = \aPayloadRho$ against payload and $\rho = \aHoverRho$ against hover power, the shared
term, and negative against altitude in \emph{every} payload stratum. Pooled across the grid the
altitude correlation is a non-significant $\aAltPooledRho$ ($p = \aAltPooledP$), because payload
spans a far larger range of hover power and swamps it; the within-stratum test is the one that
answers the question.

An eVTOL air taxi hovering at \aEvtolC{}C certifies with zero unsafe episodes, a median hover
endurance of \aEvtolMed{} minutes, and interval width predicting that endurance at
$\rho = \aEvtolRho$.

An operator planning winter service on the basis of thermal derating would be planning against
the wrong constraint. The certificate identifies the right one without being told which to look
for, because it evaluates all of them.

\subsection{What forty sorties a day actually costs}

A delivery pack lands at up to 44\,\degC{}, and the throttled margin puts the admissible ceiling
at \gCeiling\,\degC{} --- so the certificate refuses to charge it. Measuring how little charge a
refused session delivers measures the refusal. The operational question is how long the pack
must sit, and the filter answers it directly, because \emph{the wait is the time until the
interval opens}.

Of \aTurnN{} turnarounds, \aTurnImmediate\,\% could start at once; the rest waited a median of
\textbf{\aTurnWaitMed{} minutes} (95th percentile \aTurnWaitPninefive{}), and none was still
refused after two hours, with zero violations throughout. On a single pack that is
\aTurnOnePack{} sorties/day. Swapping packs so the cool-downs overlap gives \aTurnSwap{}
sorties/day, and holding a 40/day target requires \textbf{\aTurnPacks{} packs in rotation}.

This is why delivery operators swap packs rather than wait. The contribution is that the
certificate produces the number instead of the folklore.

\subsection{An adversary}

A cross-entropy adversary was given \aAdvCells{} independently drawn packs and
\aAdvSeq{} flight profiles, free to command anywhere inside the certified interval and
optimising the demand and airspeed schedule to maximise peak temperature. It reached
\aAdvPeak\,\% of the limit while the certificate was open and broke nothing: \aAdvViol{}
violations, CP-95 upper bound \aAdvCP\,\%, past the \nReqOne{} cells needed to certify below
1\,\%. The statistical unit is the \emph{cell}, not the sequence, because an earlier adversarial
claim in this line of work rested on 40 cells and bounded nothing below 7.2\,\%.

\section{Water: survey AUVs, gliders, under-ice vehicles}\label{sec:water}

The hardest domain, for reasons that have nothing to do with the theorem. A pack inside a
pressure hull is thermally isolated from an excellent heat sink --- \uHullUA\,W/K per cell
against a car's \uCarUA{}, about one \uRatio{}th --- and the water is 2\,\degC{}, so both
intuitions are wrong at once: it heats slowly \emph{and} it is very cold.

It is also the cleanest separation of the anchored generalisation from anything about flight. A
survey AUV sitting perfectly still on the seabed still cannot command zero: its computer,
inertial navigation and acoustic modem draw \uIdleW\,W regardless, an anchor of
\uIdleAnchor\,A. Across idle, survey and transit the anchored filter recorded zero
unsafe-while-certified episodes, and the null-input filter browned out the vehicle in
\textbf{\uIdleBrown{} of \uIdleN{} --- including the case where nothing is moving.}

A sealed hull certifies: \uHullN{} dock recharges in 2\,\degC{} water, \uHullViol{} violations.
A six-month deployment with no recalibration: \uDeployN{} recharges, zero violations. And 180
days with no ground truth: a fixed bound and a daily oracle record identical safety
\emph{and} identical delivered charge.

\subsection{The constraint the theorem does not certify is the one that binds}

This experiment was written to show the binding constraint \emph{switching} from thermal to
plating as the water cools. It never switches, because it was never thermal. \textbf{Plating
binds in 100\,\% of states at every water temperature from $-1.8$ to 25\,\degC{}, and
temperature binds in none} (Fig.~\ref{fig:environments}b), across \uColdTrials{} trials. A
sealed hull charges so slowly that the pack never approaches its thermal limit. What changes
with temperature is which plating mechanism binds: the temperature-dependent current cap takes
\uCapWarm\,\% of cases at 25\,\degC{} and \uCapCold\,\% at 2\,\degC{}.

That has a consequence worth stating plainly rather than dressing up. The certified set is
$\{T, V\}$; plating is enforced and monitored but never certified, because $u=0$ does not clear
the plating margin at high state of charge and so no one-step condition can make it invariant.
\textbf{On this vehicle, in this medium, the binding constraint is the one the theorem does not
certify.}

Two downstream sweeps inherit it and both come back flat. Delivered charge does not move across
ten years of dormancy (spread $\uDormSpread$ SOC) or 6\,000\,m of depth ($\uDepthSpread$),
because neither channel touches the plating cap. Those spreads are numerical noise and no rank
correlation is reported over them --- an earlier version did, and reported $\rho=+1.000$,
$p=0.0002$ for a flat line. \emph{The certificate is not tolerant of dormancy and depth so much
as blind to them.}

\subsection{A negative result, and the design rule it forces}\label{sec:ice}

Under an ice shelf there is no abort-to-surface: a closed envelope is a lost vehicle. The
question is therefore not whether the certificate is correct but whether it is \emph{early}.

\textbf{Waiting for the interval to close is too late.} Closure is the moment the envelope is
gone, so it offers no warning ahead of itself; what it does offer is the interval before the
vehicle actually breaks something, and across \uIceN{} missions that is a median of
\uIceClosureMed{} minutes. Against a \uIceTransit{}-minute transit to a known hole in the ice,
it covers the return in \uIceCover\,\% of episodes. \uIceNoWarn{} breaches occurred without a
warning --- the certificate is correct --- and correct \uIceClosureMed{} minutes before the
damage, when the vehicle needs \uIceTransit{}, loses the vehicle anyway.

The fix is not a better certificate. It is to stop using closure as the trigger. The reserve is
available at every step and falls smoothly toward zero, so the abort can fire on a
\emph{threshold} rather than on exhaustion (Table~\ref{tab:ice}). Aborting at \uIceThresh\,A of remaining reserve gives \uIceThreshMed{} minutes of warning
\emph{before} closure at the median and \uIceThreshPfive{} at the 5th percentile --- covering the
transit in every episode, with the \uIceClosureMed{}-minute post-closure margin still in hand on
top of it.

It is worth stating what this rule replaces. Current practice for autonomous underwater
endurance is a fixed reserve fraction --- turn back at 30\,\% state of charge, say --- chosen
conservatively because the quantity that actually matters, how much admissible current remains,
is not observable. A fixed SOC reserve is simultaneously too conservative in warm water with a
fresh pack and not conservative enough in cold water with an aged one, and nothing tells the
vehicle which situation it is in.

The reserve threshold is directly observable, updates every control step at
\worstLatency\,$\mu$s, and carries the temperature and ageing state implicitly because it is
computed from them. That is the difference between a mission rule that is defensible and one
that is merely traditional.

\subsection{Four decades of load, one certificate}

A buoyancy glider drawing 0.5\,W and an under-ice AUV drawing 175\,W differ by a factor of
\uLoadRatio{} in load. Both produced single-interval admissible sets in every state scanned and
zero unsafe episodes. What differs is the reserve \emph{ratio} --- \uGliderRatio{} for the
glider against \uAuvRatio{} for the AUV --- which is the dimensionless statement of how much
room each has relative to what it must draw.

\section{Vacuum: LEO, GEO, deep space, Mars, lunar night}\label{sec:space}

Space is where the certificate's least glamorous property becomes the decisive one. The filter
requires no identification of the plant: no recursive least squares, no observer to converge, no
ground station in the loop. On a car that is a convenience. On a spacecraft that has not been
touched since integration, whose ground link is hours of light time away, and whose battery has
taken \sDoseMax{}\,krad of ionising dose in the interim, \textbf{it is the only reason a fixed
pre-launch parameter set is admissible at all.}

Space is also where the \emph{form} of the guarantee matters as much as its content. Flight
software is qualified by argument about worst-case behaviour, and an iterative solver is
difficult to argue about: its run time depends on data that has not happened yet, its
convergence depends on conditioning nobody can bound in advance, and its failure mode is to
return late rather than to return wrong. A projection that always executes the same bounded
sequence of arithmetic operations, allocates nothing, and branches a fixed number of times is a
different kind of object to qualify. It is the difference between arguing that a component
usually finishes and proving that it always does.

\subsection{Four environments}

\textbf{Low Earth orbit} is defined by its count rather than its severity: at a 92.9-minute
period, \sLeoPerYear{} charge cycles a year. Five years --- \sLeoCycles{} charges, never
recalibrated --- produced \sLeoViol{} violations (CP-95 \sLeoCP\,\%), with delivered charge on
the final quarter matching the first to three decimal places.

\textbf{Geostationary orbit} is the opposite duty cycle: about ninety eclipses a year clustered
into two seasons, the longest \sGeoMax{} minutes, with a payload that is not allowed to go
quiet. Every duration out to the seasonal maximum completed, at a median depth of discharge of
\sGeoDoD\,\%, with zero unsafe episodes.

\textbf{Deep-space cruise} radiates to a 4--250\,K sink and nothing else: \sCruiseN{} draws,
\sCruiseViol{} violations, with the Stefan--Boltzmann cooling channel verified monotone in
temperature numerically rather than assumed. \textbf{The Martian surface} adds 6\,mbar of
CO$_2$ to radiation across an 80\,K diurnal swing: \sMarsTrials{} trials at hourly resolution,
\sMarsViol{} violations, with the sum of the two cooling terms confirmed monotone.

\textbf{Ionising dose} enters as one more monotone channel: zero violations to \sDoseMax{}\,krad,
with delivered charge falling gently and monotonically in dose.

\subsection{Why the count is the hard part in low Earth orbit}

The LEO number is easy to misread as routine. Fifteen and a half charge cycles a day for five
years is \sLeoCycles{} cycles executed by a filter given its parameters before launch and sent
nothing since. Over that span the pack's series resistance grows, its capacity fades and its
plating margin tightens --- and the certificate does not know any of it. It does not need to,
because a monotone \emph{bound} suffices where a point estimate would be required, and the bound
was known at integration.

The observable consequence is that delivered charge on the last quarter of the mission matches
the first to three decimal places. An operator's usual expectation is that battery performance
is a slowly degrading resource requiring periodic re-planning. Here it is flat --- not because
the pack has not aged, but because the certificate was never relying on the pack being new.

\subsection{Two inversions of terrestrial intuition}

\paragraph{Losing the radiator helps.} Sweeping $\varepsilon A$ down by two orders of magnitude
produced \sRadViol{} violations at every point --- and delivered charge \emph{rose}, from
\sRadLo{} to \sRadHi{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:environments}c). On a car, losing cooling costs charge.
Radiating to a 4--250\,K sink, a healthy radiator makes the pack \emph{cold}, series resistance
climbs, and the voltage limit arrives sooner; shrinking it lets the pack keep its own
dissipation. In this domain the pack is cold-limited, not heat-limited, and a thermal-control
failure of this kind is not an emergency.

\paragraph{Lunar night is a cold problem, so capacity is the wrong lever.} A lander on 2.9\,kWh
survives about \sNightBase{} hours of a \sNightHours{}-hour night, and quadrupling the pack buys
only a couple more --- because the envelope closes on cold-induced voltage sag, not on stored
energy. Insulation is the lever: dropping emissivity from 0.72 to \sNightBestEps{} takes
survival from \sNightBase{} to \textbf{\sNightBest{} hours}, at which point the pack stops being
cold-limited and meets its energy ceiling of \sNightEnergy{} hours. No configuration survives
the full night, which is the honest answer and the reason real landers that survive lunar night
carry radioisotope heaters rather than bigger batteries.

These two results share a structure worth naming. Terrestrial battery engineering is organised
around removing heat, because on Earth the ambient sits close to the cell's operating
temperature and the failure mode is thermal. In vacuum the ambient is 4\,K and the failure mode
inverts: the pack's problem is \emph{retaining} heat, series resistance is the mechanism, and
voltage sag is how it presents. A method tuned to terrestrial intuition --- one that treats
cooling as unambiguously good --- would prescribe exactly the wrong design for both the
deep-space probe and the lunar lander. The certificate makes no such assumption. It evaluates
the constraints it is given under the cooling law it is given, and the inversion falls out.

\subsection{No ground in the loop}

Over \sNoGroundDays{} charges --- five years --- a fixed datasheet bound and an oracle
recalibrated every single day record identical safety \emph{and} identical delivered charge: the
gap is \sNoGroundGap{} SOC points. In deep space, recalibration buys not just no safety but no
performance either, because the binding constraint is the plating corner rather than resistance.

Solar-array degradation, swept to \sArrayYears{} years --- three times any real mission, down to
\sArrayFrac\,\% of beginning-of-life output --- never becomes the binding constraint at all, and
delivered charge varies by \sArraySpread{} SOC across the whole sweep. The separation between
what the vehicle can \emph{request} and what the filter can \emph{certify} is not marginal here.

\subsection{The one fault the theorem cannot survive, measured anyway}

Every other fault model in this work corrupts the plant or the sensor. A single-event upset
corrupts the \emph{certificate}: a heavy ion flips a bit in the state word the projection is
about to read, so the filter computes an admissible interval for a spacecraft that does not
exist and commands it to the one that does.

No one-step theorem survives corruption of its own input, and claiming otherwise would be
dishonest. What can be measured is the shape of the exposure. Across \sSeuFlips{} bit flips into
state of charge, temperature and the RC overpotential: \sSeuCaught\,\% are caught by a
three-comparison range check on the state word; \sSeuBenign\,\% are benign or leave the command
\emph{more} conservative; and \sSeuAggr\,\% produce a more aggressive command than the clean
one. Of those, the worst overshoot actually realised in the plant was
\textbf{\sSeuOvershoot\,\degC{}} --- none breached anything in one step. The deliverable is the
range check and the number beside it, not a theorem.

\paragraph{Against what ships today.} A production pack is limited to a single conservative
C-rate chosen for the weakest cell at a temperature extreme at end of life, and applied whatever
the present state happens to be. The certificate delivers \textbf{$+$\bGainPts{} SOC points
($+$\bGainPct\,\%)} over that de-rate with \bViolZg{} violations. That is the entire economic
argument of \S\ref{sec:enables}, measured rather than asserted.

\paragraph{And the de-rate is not paranoia.} A comparison against a conservative baseline is
only meaningful if the conservatism is doing something. Removing it --- charging at \bAggrC{}C
on the same cells --- violates in \textbf{\bAggrViolPct\,\% of sessions}. The de-rate is
protecting against something real; the certificate's contribution is to stop paying for it when
the present state does not require it.

\paragraph{Against MPC: the same problem, solved two ways.} This is the comparison that matters
for the run-time claim, because that claim is only interesting if the solver is solving the same
problem. It is: horizon-1 MPC and the bisection agree on delivered charge to
\textbf{\bMpcGap{} SOC points}. The certificate is not a cheaper approximation of MPC --- on
this problem it is an exact solver for it.

The difference is what it costs to be sure. The bisection spends at most \bZgEval{} model
evaluations, and that is a \emph{bound}: proved in \S\ref{sec:formal}, known before any data
arrives, never exceeded. SLSQP's worst observed cost was \bMpcMaxEval{} evaluations, a factor of
\bMpcRatio{} higher --- and that number is not a bound. It is whatever the iteration cap
happened to allow, its iteration count varied by \bMpcIterSpread{} across states, and removing
the cap removes the number. A safety task slot can be sized around the first quantity and cannot
be sized around the second.

\paragraph{What this does not show.} SLSQP is a general-purpose nonlinear solver and a
specialised QP or an explicit-MPC lookup would be faster; the comparison is against the standard
approach as it is usually implemented, not against the best conceivable optimiser. What no
optimiser removes is the data-dependence: the argument here is about the \emph{shape} of the
cost, not its constant.


\section{External validation: the assumptions against measured cells}\label{sec:external}

Everything to this point is simulation. The filter is checked against a reduced-order model,
and that model was calibrated against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman simulation. That is a defensible
chain and it is still a chain of models, so the fair question is what happens when the
assumptions meet a real cell.

This section answers it with the NASA Ames Prognostics Center of Excellence battery data set:
\nCells{} 18650 cells cycled to failure with periodic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy,
published by a different laboratory, years before this work, for a different purpose. Nothing
here was produced by us.

The design point is that \textbf{the load-bearing assumptions are testable without any model at
all.} The method does not need the ROM to be right about NASA's cells. It needs three things to
be true of real cells, and each is a chemistry-independent statement about the shape of
degradation.

\subsection{The resistance bound holds}

The filter never identifies resistance; it assumes a \emph{bound} on it, taken from a datasheet
before the vehicle ships. If real cells leave that bound, every certificate in this paper is
void. Across \nEIS{} impedance measurements, \textbf{\nInsideFrac\,\% lie inside the assumed
bound $s_R = \nBound$} (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}a). Restricted to the life the certificate
actually claims --- cells at or above 80\,\% of initial capacity, the standard automotive end of
life --- the figure is \textbf{\nInsideEolFrac\,\% of \nEolMeas{} measurements}.

Over the full measured life, including cells NASA ran down to \nCapWorst{} of initial capacity,
\nViolations{} measurement exceeds the bound, on cell \nWorstCell{}, by 0.1\,\%. That is
reported rather than trimmed: the bound is tight, and it is not a comfortable over-estimate.

\subsection{Heat generation is monotone in current, measured}

Hypothesis (A2) is the other load-bearing assumption, and it too can be checked without a model
--- though the obvious way of doing it does not work, and why is worth recording. Correlating
instantaneous $\mathrm{d}T/\mathrm{d}t$ against instantaneous current returns nothing usable:
stratifying by $T-T_{\mathrm{amb}}$ to control for cooling gives rank correlations that
\emph{flip sign} between strata ($-0.34$ in the coldest, $+0.54$ in the warmest). NASA measures
surface temperature, the core leads it by minutes, and over a charge whose total rise is under
3\,K the thermal lag dominates the signal. Reporting either sign from that test would be an
artefact of the instrument.

The lag-free formulation is an energy balance over a whole cycle, which does not care how the
heat arrives: total ohmic generation goes as $\int I^2\,\mathrm{d}t$, so a cycle's peak
temperature rise should increase with it. Across \nDisCycles{} measured discharge cycles, with
currents to \nMaxI\,A and temperature rises to \nMaxDT\,K, the rank correlation is
\textbf{$\rho = \nDisRho$} ($p < 0.0005$; Fig.~\ref{fig:external}b). The charge cycles give
$\nChgRho$, which is weaker for a mundane reason: NASA charges almost everything at a constant
1.5\,A, so there is little current variation to correlate against.

\subsection{Where the model does not transfer, and whether that matters}

The harder question is how the ROM itself behaves on a cell it was never fitted to --- a 5\,Ah
NMC model applied to $\sim$2\,Ah LCO cells with capacity rescaled and nothing else refitted.
Measured one step ahead the question is meaningless, because NASA samples every $\sim$9\,s over
which the temperature moves about 0.01\,K and predicting ``unchanged'' scores well. Integrated
forward over a \nHorizon\,s horizon under the measured current, the transferred model scores
RMSE \nRMSE\,\degC{} against a persistence baseline of \nPersist\,\degC{} --- a skill of
\nSkill{}.

The safety-relevant question is not the magnitude but the \emph{sign}. A model that
over-predicts temperature yields a conservative certificate; one that under-predicts yields an
unsafe one. \textbf{This transfer is not conservative}: the bias is \nBias\,\degC{} and it
over-predicts in only \nOverFrac\,\% of forecasts. What rescues it is the margin. The worst
under-prediction at the 5th percentile is \nUnderWorst\,\degC{} against a thermal margin of
12.5\,\degC{} --- a factor of \textbf{\nMarginCover} of cover (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}c). The
margin was not designed for cross-chemistry transfer; it happens to absorb it, and that is a
fact about this data rather than a guarantee.

\subsection{The filter permits a real protocol, and refuses where it said it would}

A safety filter that refuses everything is safe and useless. Given the end-of-life resistance
bound and the full throttled margin --- its most conservative configuration --- the certificate
permits \textbf{\nPermitFrac\,\%} of NASA's measured charge steps, with a median headroom of
\nHeadroom$\times$ the applied current. Of the refusals, \nRefuseCeil\,\% are cells measured
above the throttled ceiling of \nCeiling\,\degC{}: the same frontier \S\ref{sec:ground}
predicts from the margin alone, appearing unprompted in someone else's measurements.

\subsection{One test that did not work}

It was tempting to go further and ask whether the cells the filter would have refused are the
cells that actually degraded. The mechanism would have been clean --- NASA cycled some cells at
4\,\degC{}, the plating cap falls to a 0.70C floor below about 5.5\,\degC{}, and plating is what
degrades a cold-charged cell.

The association is not stable. Its sign \emph{changes} with how the capacity baseline is
defined --- first measured discharge against the median of the largest few --- because NASA's
first discharge on \nBadBaselines{} of \nCapCells{} cells is partial, giving baselines as low as
0.068\,Ah for a 2\,Ah cell. Across the four defensible combinations of baseline and fade metric,
$\rho$ ranges from $\nAssocLo$ to $\nAssocHi$, with significant results of both signs, and
ambient temperature and cycle count sit behind both quantities. All four are reported in
Table-form in the results file rather than one. \textbf{A result that flips sign with a
defensible change of definition is not a result}, and selecting the flattering version would be
the most misleading thing this study could do.

\section{What makes this one method}\label{sec:cross}

Forty-four experiments establish that the certificate holds in each medium. That is not the same
as establishing it is the \emph{same} certificate --- four domains that each worked for their
own reasons would be four papers. Five cross-cutting checks, each of which would fail if the
domains were only superficially related.

\textbf{The admissible set is a single interval everywhere.} \scanTotal{} dense scans across all
\vPlatforms{} platforms and both modes: \scanSingle{} single intervals, \scanEmpty{} empty,
\textbf{\scanDisconnected{} disconnected} --- measured, not argued from monotonicity.

\textbf{The generalisation reduces to the original theorem exactly.} Bit-exact on
\reduceStates{} states, \reduceMismatch{} mismatches, and every charge-mode platform's lower
edge is exactly zero: Proposition~\ref{prop:nic} recovered, not approximated.

\textbf{The cost is fixed, and it is the same fixed cost everywhere.} \evalCharge{} evaluations
on every charge platform and \evalDischarge{} on every discharge platform, against theoretical
bounds of exactly \evalCharge{} and \evalDischarge{}. Worst p99 latency across all sixteen is
\worstLatency\,$\mu$s, or \slotFraction\,\% of a 10\,ms task slot.

\textbf{Every zero-failure claim is backed by enough samples.} Twelve headline claims,
\adequacyTrials{} pooled trials, \adequacyViol{} violations; all exceed the \nReqOne{} trials
needed to certify below 1\,\% at 95\,\% confidence and five exceed the \nReqTenth{} for
0.1\,\%.

\subsection{The reserve is one of two clocks}

The claim that interval width is a countdown survives in the air and on the Moon and does
\emph{not} survive underwater, and pooling would have hidden it (Table~\ref{tab:clocks}).

An interval closes for one of two reasons. \emph{Envelope-driven} closure is the upper edge
falling onto the lower one as the pack heats and sags --- there the width \emph{is} the distance
still to travel. \emph{Energy-driven} closure is the state of charge reaching its floor with the
interval still wide open --- there the clock is set by the charge that was in the pack, and the
width says nothing about it. Both are correctly reported as closures; only the first is a
countdown. The certificate carries both quantities and every platform has one of them
significantly positive ($\rho>0.3$, $p<0.001$), the \reserveWeakest{} most weakly at
$\reserveWeakestRho$. \textbf{The rule is to act on the minimum of the two}, which is
conservative whether or not either is individually tight. The pooled, anchor-normalised
correlation is $\reservePooledRho$ over $\reservePooledN{}$ episodes; reporting that alone would
have been the most flattering presentation available and the least honest.

\section{What this enables}\label{sec:enables}

The results above are safety results; their significance is what they permit. What follows
separates what has been demonstrated from what follows from it, and attaches a number to each
where one exists.

\paragraph{A vehicle can charge at the limit that applies to it, not the limit that applies to
the worst vehicle.} A production pack's de-rate is a single number chosen for end of life, at a
temperature extreme, on the weakest cell. A vehicle that is warm, mid-life and mid-charge is
held to it anyway, because nothing on board can prove it deserves better. The certificate is
that proof, evaluated at the current state under a bound that is still conservative, so nothing
is traded away. \S\ref{sec:ground} measures one slice of the difference --- \gLoopGain{} points
of delivered charge between a pack coupled to ambient and one with a loop --- and shows the
frontier is sharp and predictable, at $T_{\max}-\delta_T = \gCeiling\,\degC$ rather than
somewhere discovered empirically.

\paragraph{Aircraft and packs can be sized before they are built.} The takeoff frontier of
\S\ref{sec:aerial} is a design curve, not a diagnostic: 6S2P cannot certify a $1.75\times$
takeoff transient, 6S3P certifies it and completes \aSortieComplete\,\% of sorties, 6S4P
completes \aSortieBigger\,\%. The refusal of \S\ref{sec:refusal} goes further and rejects a cell
\emph{chemistry} for a mission class in a single evaluation, before an airframe exists. A guard
that only ever answers questions about the vehicle it is installed in cannot do either.

\paragraph{Fleet capital planning gets an equation instead of a rule of thumb.} A delivery
operator asking what it costs to fly forty sorties a day is asking a battery question, and
\S\ref{sec:aerial} answers it: \aTurnImmediate\,\% of packs can charge on landing, the rest wait
a median of \aTurnWaitMed{} minutes, one pack sustains \aTurnOnePack{} sorties/day, and holding
the target requires \textbf{\aTurnPacks{} packs per airframe}. That is a procurement number
derived from a thermal margin, and it was previously folklore.

\paragraph{Vehicles that cannot be rescued get an abort rule with a number in it.}
\S\ref{sec:ice} replaces ``turn back when you are low'' --- a fixed state-of-charge reserve that
is simultaneously too cautious in warm water with a fresh pack and not cautious enough in cold
water with an aged one --- with ``abort at \uIceThresh\,A of remaining reserve'', which gives
\uIceThreshPfive{} minutes of warning at the 5th percentile and covers the return in every
episode tested. The threshold carries temperature and ageing implicitly because it is computed
from them.

\paragraph{A mission can fly a fixed parameter set for a decade and know exactly what it cost.}
\S\ref{sec:space} runs \sLeoCycles{} LEO cycles and \sNoGroundDays{} deep-space charges against
an oracle recalibrated daily, and measures the price of never recalibrating at
\sNoGroundGap{} SOC points. For a spacecraft that cannot be recalibrated in any case, the
significant part is not that the price is small; it is that the guarantee does not depend on
paying it.

\paragraph{And the design space itself becomes searchable.} Three results in this paper are
design conclusions that fell out of running the certificate over a \emph{sweep} rather than a
trajectory: a rotorcraft's binding constraint below freezing is voltage, not temperature
(\S\ref{sec:aerial}); a sealed hull is plating-limited at every water temperature
(\S\ref{sec:water}); and a lunar lander's survival is bought by emissivity, \sNightBase{} to
\sNightBest{} hours, not by capacity (\S\ref{sec:space}). Each required only asking the
certificate about a vehicle that does not exist yet, at \worstLatency\,$\mu$s per answer ---
deterministic, branch-bounded arithmetic that allocates nothing, calls no library, and has no
path on which it fails to terminate. There is no solver to qualify and no iteration cap to
justify, which matters most exactly where qualification is hardest.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

Stated in full, because several of them are load-bearing.

\paragraph{No high-fidelity electrochemistry in this study.} The simulated results are
reduced-order-model level or model-versus-model; the Doyle--Fuller--Newman validation of the
underlying filter is prior work \cite{krishnan2026safe}, cited rather than re-derived.
\S\ref{sec:external} is the exception and does not close the gap: it validates the
\emph{assumptions} against measured cells, not the vehicle results.

\paragraph{The certified set is $\{T, V\}$; plating is enforced, never certified.}
\S\ref{sec:cell} gives the reason and \S\ref{sec:water} shows it is not a technicality: in a
sealed hull at any water temperature, the binding constraint is the one the theorem does not
certify. Extending to plating needs a control-invariance argument the $u=0$ backup cannot
supply, and we do not have one.

\paragraph{One cell, sixteen vehicles.} Every platform uses the coefficients of
\S\ref{sec:cell}, because that is the cell the model was fitted to. Where a class would really
use a different cell --- the rotorcraft --- the substitution is explicit ($0.40\times$
resistance, $0.60\times$ capacity), is an extrapolation beyond the calibration, and every aerial
number carries it.

\paragraph{Class-representative platforms.} Pack geometry, hover powers, hotel loads and orbit
parameters place each vehicle in the right region of its class's design space; they are not any
manufacturer's design.

\paragraph{No theorem survives corruption of its own input.} \S\ref{sec:space} measures the
single-event-upset exposure and offers a range check; it does not claim robustness to a flipped
bit, because there is none to claim.

\paragraph{The vehicle results remain simulation.} \S\ref{sec:external} tests the assumptions
against measured cells, but every \emph{vehicle} number in \S\ref{sec:ground}--\S\ref{sec:space}
comes from a plant we wrote, and a filter tested against models chosen to satisfy its hypotheses
is weaker than one tested on hardware. Three things partially answer it: three of the four media
\emph{break} the original hypothesis, one platform is refused outright, and fourteen of
\vExperiments{} experiments contradicted the hypothesis they were written to test --- the
register was not built so that everything passes. Running the filter in the loop on real cells
is the natural next step and is not claimed here.

\paragraph{The external validation is of assumptions, not of vehicles.} NASA's cells are $\sim$2\,Ah
LCO 18650s charged at 1.5\,A; none of them is a robotaxi, a rotorcraft or a spacecraft. What
\S\ref{sec:external} establishes is that the two hypotheses the theorem consumes hold on real
cells, and that the ROM's transfer error is small against the margin --- not that any vehicle
result would reproduce on hardware.

\section{Conclusion}

The safety guarantee an autonomous vehicle's battery needs turns out to require neither an
optimiser nor an identified model, only a geometric fact: when constraints are monotone in a
scalar input and one input is known safe, the admissible set is an interval, and an interval can
be found by bisection at fixed cost.

Getting that from a cell to a vehicle required noticing that the theorem's apparent failure in
flight was a misreading. Zero current is still safe for every temperature and voltage constraint
on a discharging pack; what zero cannot do is serve the load the vehicle exists to serve, and
that is a floor rather than a cap. Anchored Collapse adds the floor family and a second
bisection, and nothing else --- \evalDischarge{} fixed evaluations against the original
\evalCharge{}.

Across \vPlatforms{} platforms in four media and \vExperiments{} experiments totalling
\vTotalEpisodes{} episodes, nothing was breached while the certificate reported feasible, the
admissible set was a single interval in all \scanTotal{} dense scans, and the worst latency
anywhere was \worstLatency\,$\mu$s.

The results we would most want a reader to take are the ones that did not come out as intended.
A certificate that refuses a cell chemistry for a mission class before takeoff is doing
something a runtime guard is not normally asked to do. A domain in which the binding constraint
is the one the theorem explicitly does not certify is a limitation a less careful register would
not have surfaced. And a warning that leaves \uIceClosureMed{} minutes when the vehicle
needs \uIceTransit{} is a negative result whose forced abort rule --- act on a threshold in the
reserve, not on its exhaustion --- is more useful than the positive result would have been.
