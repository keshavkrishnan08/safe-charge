\begin{abstract}
\noindent
An autonomous vehicle's battery must be pushed hard enough to be useful and held back enough to
be safe, and the state deciding where that line falls --- series resistance, plating margin, true
capacity --- is not measurable in the field. Every unattended vehicle inherits the same
compromise: a fixed de-rate chosen offline for the worst cell at the worst temperature at end of
life. The alternatives are to identify the pack online, which needs excitation an operational
vehicle cannot inject, or to solve a constrained optimisation each step, whose iteration count a
safety task slot cannot be sized around.

We prove that neither is necessary. Terminal voltage, temperature and the lithium-plating
potential are each \emph{monotone} in applied current, so the admissible input set is a single
\textbf{interval} whose edges are found by bisection in a \emph{fixed} number of one-step model
evaluations. The published form assumes the safe input is zero, which holds for a cell being
charged and fails for any vehicle doing its job: zero satisfies every temperature and voltage
constraint on a discharging pack, but it cannot serve the load, which is a \emph{floor} rather
than a cap. \textbf{Anchored Collapse} adds the floor family and a second bisection ---
\evalDischarge{} fixed evaluations against \evalCharge.

What that buys is not speed but \emph{transfer}. A de-rate tuned on a fleet a manufacturer could
characterise in advance violates in \bThreeTunedViolPct\,\% of deployment sessions; the
certificate, having nothing to tune, violates in \bThreeZgViol{} of \bThreeTrials. Against the
control-barrier-function quadratic program the relationship is closer than rivalry: enforced on
the true model rather than a linearisation, the discrete-time CBF condition for a monotone
constraint \emph{is} an interval, so this is the bisection that solves that problem exactly.
And a Lagrangian-constrained policy that meets its violation budget in training
(\rlHardTrain\,\%) violates in \rlHardDeploy\,\% of deployment sessions --- an expectation over
one distribution is not a per-step guarantee on another.

The theorem is \textbf{machine-checked in Lean~4} (\leanThms{} theorems, no \texttt{sorry},
\leanFreeWord{} of them axiom-free), and the proof needs less than we claimed: safety holds for an \emph{arbitrary}
predicate, so monotonicity buys optimality, not safety. The assumptions are tested against
\nCells{} cells measured by another laboratory and the filter against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman
plant in both directions. It compiles to \fwText\,B and \fwRam\,B of RAM, worst case
\fwUsMax\,$\mu$s.

Across \vPlatforms{} platforms in four media and \vExperiments{} experiments, nothing was
breached while the certificate reported feasible. \textbf{Twenty-one of those experiments
contradicted the hypothesis they were written to test} and are reported as findings --- among
them two media of four where the incumbent is \emph{not} beaten, and a cycle-life benefit that
plating prevention makes tempting to claim and that DFN degradation does not support.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The limiting resource for long-duration autonomy is not perception, planning, or control. It is
\emph{energy under certainty}: how hard a vehicle may push its battery when nobody is available
to inspect it, and how confident anyone can be in the answer.

Fast charging drives three coupled failure modes --- thermal runaway, over-potential, and lithium
plating on the anode --- whose onset depends on internal states a production sensor suite does not
measure. Series resistance roughly doubles over a pack's service life, plating risk climbs as
temperature falls, and the safe current at end of life in cold weather is a fraction of the safe
current when new and warm. A manufacturer facing that uncertainty does the only defensible thing
and de-rates for the worst case, permanently.

For a privately owned car that is a mild inefficiency. For an autonomous fleet it is the
economics of the enterprise: a robotaxi charging twelve times a day accumulates a private car's
decade of fast-charge stress in \gWeeks{} weeks, and the de-rate it inherits was designed for a
vehicle with a different duty cycle. For a spacecraft it is worse, because the de-rate is chosen
before launch and can never be revisited.

The two standard alternatives do not reach the vehicle. \emph{Identification} --- estimate the
pack's state online and de-rate against the estimate --- needs excitation an operational vehicle
is not free to inject, and makes the guarantee conditional on an estimator having converged,
which is a claim about a transient nobody audits. \emph{Constrained optimisation} --- solve a
small model-predictive control problem each step --- is the standard modern answer with a
specific defect: a quadratic program's iteration count depends on its data, and a safety-critical
task slot sized for an unbounded worst case cannot be sized at all.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A geometric fact, and its consequence.} Voltage, temperature and plating potential
are monotone in current, so the admissible input set is an \emph{interval}. Its edges are found
by bisection at a cost fixed before run time: \evalCharge{} model evaluations on charge,
\evalDischarge{} on discharge. No optimiser, no convergence test, no identified model.

\item \textbf{Anchored Collapse.} The published theorem assumes the safe input is zero, which is
true of a cell on a bench and false of any vehicle serving a load. Splitting the constraints into
caps and floors and running a second bisection removes that assumption, and is what carries the
result from a charger to a rotorcraft, an AUV and a spacecraft.

\item \textbf{It is the exact CBF filter, not a rival to it.} Enforced on the true model rather
than a linearisation, the discrete-time control-barrier-function condition for a monotone
constraint defines an interval. This work is the bisection that solves it exactly, with $\gamma$
entering as a margin; the quadratic program people deploy is its linearised approximation.

\item \textbf{Machine-checked, and the hypothesis is proved sharp.} \leanThms{} theorems in
Lean~4, no \texttt{sorry}, \leanFreeWord{} depending on no axiom at all. Monotonicity is proved
\emph{necessary} for optimality, not merely sufficient. Writing them showed that safety holds for an
\emph{arbitrary} predicate: monotonicity buys optimality, not safety.

\item \textbf{The guarantee transfers where a tuned constant does not.} Held to a
tune-then-deploy standard, the incumbent rule fails in two of four media and the certificate in
none --- and in the other two the incumbent is \emph{not} beaten, which
\S\ref{sec:cross} predicts in advance from whether mission closure is envelope- or energy-driven.

\item \textbf{Checked against physics we did not write, and a compiler.} The assumptions against
\nCells{} cells from another laboratory; the filter against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman plant, where
the affine plating proxy held the \emph{actual} electrode potential above the deposition onset;
and the implementation as \fwText\,B of compiled C agreeing with the reference at all
\fwStates{} states tested.

\item \textbf{A register that was not written to pass.} \vExperiments{} experiments,
\vTotalEpisodes{} episodes; twenty of them contradicted the hypothesis they were written to test
and are reported as findings rather than removed (Appendix).
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The framework is control invariance \cite{blanchini2008settheoretic}. Control barrier functions
\cite{ames2019cbf} and predictive safety filters \cite{wabersich2021psf} enforce it by solving an
optimisation each step; reference governors \cite{gilbert2002governor} and shielding
\cite{alshiekh2018shielding} are related, and constrained reinforcement learning
\cite{achiam2017cpo,altman1999constrained,stooke2020pid} offers guarantees on the
\emph{expectation} of violations. Ours is a predictive safety filter whose optimisation has been
replaced by a bisection, which is possible only because of monotonicity
\cite{angeli2003monotone,coogan2015mixed} --- order-preserving structure that lets worst-case
reachability be computed from extreme points rather than by search. \S\ref{sec:cbf} and
\S\ref{sec:crl} measure against the first and the last of those rather than arguing with them.

On the battery side the reference model is Doyle--Fuller--Newman \cite{doyle1993,newman2004} with
Bernardi thermal coupling \cite{bernardi1985}, in PyBaMM \cite{sulzer2021pybamm} with the Chen
\cite{chen2020} and O'Kane \cite{okane2022} parameter sets; plating is the dominant fast-charge
degradation mode \cite{yang2017plating}. Online estimation of resistance and capacity is mature
\cite{plett2015,moura2017} --- and is exactly what this method deliberately does not require.
Reinforcement learning has been applied to fast charging \cite{wei2022ddpg,attia2020}. Our
position is that the policy may be learned and the safety must not be.

\section{The cell, and which of its constraints are monotone}\label{sec:cell}

The result of \S\ref{sec:theory} is a statement about constraints, so it is worth writing the
constraints down. Everything in this paper rests on a reduced-order electro-thermal model of a
lithium-ion cell, calibrated against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman simulation in PyBaMM and reported
in \cite{krishnan2026safe} at a held-out voltage RMSE of 20.3\,mV and temperature RMSE of
1.32\,\degC{} across ambients from 0 to 25\,\degC{}. The model is prior work; what matters here
is its \emph{shape}, because that is what the theorem consumes.

\paragraph{State and outputs.} The state is $x = (\mathrm{soc}, T, V_1)$ --- state of charge,
cell temperature, and the overpotential stored in a single RC branch --- with a slowly varying
ageing pair (capacity loss, resistance growth) carried alongside. For an applied current $I$
(positive charging) over a step $\Delta t$, the model returns terminal voltage, the next
temperature, and a plating-potential proxy:
\begin{align}
V &= \mathrm{OCV}(\mathrm{soc}) \;+\; \underbrace{I\,R_\Omega(\mathrm{soc},T)}_{\text{ohmic}}
   \;+\; \underbrace{A\big(1+c_T (T-25)\big)\,\mathrm{asinh}\!\big(I/2i_0(\mathrm{soc})\big)}_{\text{activation}}
   \;+\; V_1, \label{eq:V}\\[2pt]
C_{\mathrm{th}}\,\dot T &= \underbrace{I\,\eta_\Omega + I\,\eta_{\mathrm{act}}
   + V_1^2/R_1}_{\text{irreversible}} \;+\; \underbrace{I\,(T+273.15)\,\partial U/\partial T}_{\text{reversible}}
   \;-\; q_{\mathrm{out}}(T), \label{eq:T}\\[2pt]
\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} &= p_0(\mathrm{soc}) \;-\; p_1(\mathrm{soc},T)\,I, \qquad p_1 > 0.
\label{eq:phi}
\end{align}
$R_\Omega$ carries an Arrhenius temperature dependence, so resistance rises as the cell cools;
$q_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the cooling law, and it is the \emph{only} thing that changes between the
four media of \S\ref{sec:domains}.

\paragraph{Why each constraint is monotone in current.} This is the property the theorem needs,
and each one has a physical reason rather than an empirical one.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Voltage} \eqref{eq:V}. Both overpotential terms are odd and increasing in $I$
($\mathrm{asinh}$ is strictly increasing), and $\mathrm{OCV}$ does not depend on $I$. So $V$
increases in charge current and decreases in discharge current: a cap on charge, a cap on
discharge as well, because the constraint there is $V \ge V_{\min}$ on a signal that falls.
\item \textbf{Temperature} \eqref{eq:T}. Every generation term is non-decreasing in $|I|$ ---
$I\eta_\Omega = I^2 R_\Omega \ge 0$ regardless of sign --- and $q_{\mathrm{out}}$ does not
depend on $I$ at all. So the next temperature is non-decreasing in current, whatever the cooling
law, which is why replacing Newtonian convection with Stefan--Boltzmann radiation
(\S\ref{sec:domains}) leaves the certificate's structure untouched.
\item \textbf{Plating} \eqref{eq:phi} is \emph{affine} and strictly decreasing in $I$. Plating
occurs when $\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} \le 0$.
\item \textbf{Delivered power} $P = S\,V(I)\,I$ is increasing in $I$ below the maximum-power
point --- the one condition of Remark~\ref{rem:mpp}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why plating is enforced and not certified.} This distinction recurs throughout the
paper and it is a consequence of \eqref{eq:phi} rather than a modelling choice. Forward
invariance requires an always-available input that returns the state to the safe set. At $I=0$,
$\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} = p_0(\mathrm{soc})$, and $p_0$ falls with state of charge: above roughly
0.82\,SOC it is already below the required margin. \textbf{There is no current --- not even zero
--- that clears the plating margin at high state of charge}, so no one-step condition can render
it invariant, and no honest certificate can claim it.

The certified set is therefore $\mathcal{S} = \{T \le T_{\max},\; V \le V_{\max}\}$, and plating
is \emph{enforced} by the same bisection plus a temperature-dependent current cap, and monitored.
It rides the same monotone structure, so it costs nothing extra to enforce; it simply cannot be
promised. \S\ref{sec:domains} is where that distinction stops being bookkeeping and becomes the
binding constraint of an entire vehicle class.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

Let a system have state $x$, scalar input $u \in [u_{\min}, u_{\max}]$, one-step map
$x^+ = f(x,u,w)$, and a finite family of constraints $c_j(x,u)\le 0$ defining a safe set
$\mathcal{S}$. Write $\mathcal{U}(x) = \{u : c_j(x,u)\le 0\ \forall j\}$ for the admissible input
set.

\subsection{Null-Input Collapse}

\begin{proposition}[Null-Input Collapse]\label{prop:nic}
Suppose \textbf{(A1)} $u = 0$ is safe --- from every $x \in \mathcal{S}$ the one-step map at
$u=0$ returns to $\mathcal{S}$ --- and \textbf{(A2)} each $c_j$ is non-decreasing in $u$. Then
$\mathcal{U}(x) = [0, u^\ast(x)]$ is a single interval anchored at zero, $\mathcal{S}$ is
forward-invariant under any policy filtered through it, and $u^\ast$ is computed to precision
$\varepsilon$ in $\lceil \log_2 (u_{\max}/\varepsilon)\rceil + 2$ one-step evaluations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By (A2) each $\{u : c_j \le 0\}$ is a down-set; a finite intersection of down-sets in
$\mathbb{R}$ is a down-set, hence an interval $[u_{\min}, u^\ast]$, which by (A1) contains $0$.
Bisection on a monotone predicate converges linearly with an iteration count depending only on
the requested precision, not on the data. Forward invariance follows by induction: each filtered
step lands in $\mathcal{S}$ and $u=0$ remains available.
\end{proof}

Two things are worth isolating. First, no step requires knowing the plant's uncertain
parameters --- only evaluating $c_j$ under a \emph{bound} on them, and a conservative bound
yields a conservative interval. Second, the cost is $O(\log 1/\varepsilon)$ \emph{deterministic}
evaluations, which is what a task slot can be sized around and a solver's iteration count is
not.

\subsection{Where it breaks, and why that reading is wrong}\label{sec:anchored}

Placed on a quadrotor holding altitude, a filter built on Proposition~\ref{prop:nic}
\cite{krishnan2026safe} passes
every one-step check for a mean of 22.3 consecutive steps and then violates in 100\,\% of
episodes. The natural reading is that (A1) has failed: $u=0$ is plainly not safe for an
aircraft.

That reading is wrong, and the correction is the technical heart of this paper. Consider what
$u=0$ actually does to a discharging pack. No current flows, so there is no $I^2R$ heating and
the temperature relaxes toward ambient. Terminal voltage returns to open-circuit, so it cannot
sag below a floor. State of charge does not fall. \textbf{Every temperature and voltage
constraint is satisfied at $u=0$, on a quadrotor in flight exactly as on a cell being charged.}

What $u = 0$ cannot do is fly the aircraft. The constraint that failed is of a different kind:
a requirement that the input be \emph{large} enough. Call these \emph{floors}, as against the
\emph{caps} of Proposition~\ref{prop:nic}. Every vehicle has at least one, because every vehicle
has an irreducible load --- the power it cannot stop drawing and remain a vehicle. Hover thrust
for a rotorcraft. Hotel load for a submersible, whose computer and inertial navigation draw
their watts whether the thrusters do or not, so that even a stationary AUV cannot command zero.
Bus load for a spacecraft in eclipse. Contracted export power for a car doing vehicle-to-grid.

Charging is the special case where the irreducible load is zero --- which is exactly why the
original filter could anchor at zero without ever noticing it was making a choice.

\begin{theorem}[Anchored Collapse]\label{thm:anchored}
Partition the constraints into caps $\mathcal{C}$, each satisfied on $[u_{\min}, u_c]$, and
floors $\mathcal{F}$, each satisfied on $[u_c, u_{\max}]$. Suppose
\textbf{(A1$'$)} $u = u_{\min}$ is safe for every cap, from every $x \in \mathcal{S}$, and
\textbf{(A2)} each constraint is monotone in $u$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{U}(x) \;=\; [\,u_{\mathrm{lo}}(x),\; u_{\mathrm{hi}}(x)\,]
\;=\;\Big[\;\max_{j\in\mathcal{F}} u_{c_j},\;\; \min_{j\in\mathcal{C}} u_{c_j}\;\Big],
\end{equation}
both edges are found by bisection in $O(\log 1/\varepsilon)$ one-step evaluations, and
$\mathcal{U}(x)\neq\emptyset$ exactly when $u_{\mathrm{lo}} \le u_{\mathrm{hi}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Each cap's satisfying set is a down-set and each floor's an up-set; their finite intersections
are $[u_{\min}, u_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ and $[u_{\mathrm{lo}}, u_{\max}]$, whose intersection is the
stated interval. (A1$'$) places $u_{\min}$ inside the cap family, giving the upper bisection a
known-good left endpoint; $u_{\max}$ is inside the floor family if anything is, giving the lower
bisection its right endpoint. Neither bisection needs the load current as a seed.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:nic} is the case $\mathcal{F} = \emptyset$. Note that (A1$'$) is
\emph{strictly weaker} than (A1): it asks only that zero satisfy the caps, which is the part
that survives contact with a vehicle.

\begin{remark}[The side of a constraint is data, not inference]
The plating constraint is $\varphi_{\mathrm{an}} \ge \delta$ --- a lower-bound test on a signal
that \emph{decreases} in current, so it caps current from above. Sense and side coincide only
when the constrained signal increases in $u$. Inferring side from the operator would file that
cap among the floors and let the filter command straight through it: silent, and unsafe. Plants
declare the side; an undeclared constraint raises rather than defaults.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A soundness condition that is not obvious]\label{rem:mpp}
Delivered bus power $P = S\,V(u)\,u$ with $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}u<0$ peaks at the maximum-power
point and \emph{falls} beyond it, so past that peak the set where the load is met stops being an
up-set and the lower bisection would search a non-monotone family. The voltage cap prevents it:
a pack reaches its maximum-power point near half its open-circuit voltage, far below any usable
$V_{\min}$, so $u_{\mathrm{hi}} < u_{\mathrm{MPP}}$ always and the floor search is bounded by
$u_{\mathrm{hi}}$. This ordering is checked on every discharge platform, not assumed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Machine-checked}\label{sec:formal}

Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored} and the soundness of the bisection are formalised in Lean~4 and
checked mechanically (\texttt{formal/AnchoredCollapse.lean}). \leanThms{} theorems compile
against \texttt{\leanToolchain} with \leanSorry{} occurrences of \texttt{sorry} and no mathlib
dependency; the numbers here are read out of the build by
\texttt{zeroguard/exp/l1\_lean\_audit.py} rather than transcribed, so they cannot drift from the
artifact.

The axiom audit is per theorem because it is not uniform. \textbf{\leanFreeWord{} of the
\leanThms{} depend on no axioms whatsoever} --- not classical choice, not propositional
extensionality. The remaining \leanUsing{} depend on \leanAxioms{} alone, entering through core's
\texttt{Nat} division lemmas. Nothing anywhere depends on \texttt{Classical.choice} or
\texttt{sorryAx}. The proofs are stated over an arbitrary type carrying a $\le$ relation with no
order axiom used, so they hold without transitivity, antisymmetry or totality.

\paragraph{Writing them weakened the hypotheses, and then sharpened them.} The safety guarantee
turns out never to touch the cap/floor structure at all: \texttt{bisect\_sound\_any\_predicate}
holds for an \emph{arbitrary} predicate, monotone or not, connected or not. So (A2) buys
\textbf{optimality, not safety}.

That statement was an observation. It is now a pair of theorems, and together they say the
hypothesis is exactly the right one rather than merely a convenient one.

\emph{With} monotonicity, \texttt{bisect\_brackets\_edge} proves the search \emph{brackets} the
true edge: everything at or below the returned point is admissible and everything at or above
the upper endpoint is not, so the entire error is the bracket width. That is what converts a
fixed iteration count into a fixed \emph{accuracy} --- \evalCharge{} evaluations do not merely
terminate, they localise the edge to $2^{-18}$ of the actuator range.

\emph{Without} it, the answer can be arbitrarily poor while remaining perfectly safe. A
constraint feasible at $0$ and at $4$ and nowhere between --- a plant with two disjoint
operating bands --- is proved outside the hypothesis, and the bisection is proved to return $0$
while $4$ is admissible, at \emph{every} budget up to 24 iterations. It cannot recover: every
probe between the bands reports infeasible, so nothing in the transcript says a second band
exists. More compute does not help, because the gap is a property of the constraint rather than
of the budget.

That is the practical statement for anyone applying this to a plant whose structure they have
not verified: you may lose charge, you cannot lose the guarantee, and the amount you can lose is
unbounded.

The property worth checking mechanically is not the collapse theorem but
\texttt{bisect\_sound}: \emph{every} state the bisection can reach has a feasible lower
endpoint. Convergence says how good the answer is; this says whether it is safe, and it holds
independent of iteration count, of the midpoint rule, and of floating-point behaviour --- the
proof never assumes the midpoint is the arithmetic mean, only that it lies between the
endpoints. A filter interrupted by a deadline, starved of its budget, or run with a sloppy
division still returns an admissible input.

\subsection{Cost}

Two probes bracket the cap family, then eighteen halvings; two more bracket the floors, then
eighteen more. Worst case \evalDischarge{} one-step model evaluations on discharge and
\evalCharge{} on charge, both fixed and both branch-bounded. Measured worst p99 latency across
all \vPlatforms{} platforms is \worstLatency{}\,$\mu$s (\worstLatencyCase{}), which is
\slotFraction\,\% of a 10\,ms ISO~26262 task slot. On the ground platforms the figure is
\groundLatency{}\,$\mu$s, or \groundSlotPct\,\%.

The property that matters is not that the number is small. It is that it does not move with the
data.

\subsection{The reserve}\label{sec:reserve}

The two-sided certificate makes available a quantity the one-sided version cannot express: the
width $u_{\mathrm{hi}} - u_{\mathrm{lo}}$ shrinks to zero as the envelope closes, so the filter
can report \emph{how close to unrecoverable} a vehicle is before it arrives. We treat this claim
adversarially in \S\ref{sec:domains} and scope it in \S\ref{sec:cross}: it carries the most
operational weight and is the most likely to be overstated.

\section{Method and platforms}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The filter}

Given a proposed input $u_{\mathrm{req}}$, the filter returns
$\mathrm{clip}(u_{\mathrm{req}}, u_{\mathrm{lo}}, u_{\mathrm{hi}})$, or reports the interval
empty. Empty is not a violation: it is the certificate stating correctly that the irreducible
load can no longer be served inside the envelope, which is the true statement and the one a
mission planner needs.

Robustness enters through margins rather than estimation: a thermal throttle
$\delta_T = \delta_{T0} + K(s_R - 1)$ scaled by the \emph{assumed} resistance bound $s_R$, with
$K$ the smallest value certifying cells past the rated bound, and a cooling reserve $f$ that
certifies any cooling loss up to $f/(1+f)$.

cooling law; the \textbf{pack}, through series/parallel counts; and the \textbf{load}, through
the irreducible draw. Table~\ref{tab:platforms} lists them.

The cell underneath is the reduced-order model of \cite{krishnan2026safe} in every case --- same
overpotential form, same plating proxy, same calibrated coefficients --- with two changes the
vehicles require. Current may be negative, because vehicles discharge. And the Newtonian cooling
term is a callable, because a quadrotor at 400\,m, an AUV at 2\,\degC{} and a spacecraft in
vacuum do not share a cooling law. Under the Newtonian law with positive current the vehicle
cell reproduces the published model \emph{bit for bit}, and every result downstream inherits
that.

Pack geometry, hover powers, hotel loads and orbit parameters are
\emph{class-representative} --- placing each vehicle in the right region of its class's design
space using published figures --- and are not any manufacturer's design. The purpose is that
``it works on autonomous vehicles'' is not a testable sentence, while ``it holds a 96S45P pack
under a 30-minute 350\,kW session at 35\,\degC{} ambient'' is.

\subsection{Experimental discipline}

Every experiment runs a \emph{pessimistic estimator against a drawn plant}: the filter is given a
platform at the conservative corner of the parameter envelope, and the plant it drives is drawn
from inside it. A filter tested against its own model is testing arithmetic. One property makes
that corner well defined for a two-sided certificate --- scaling the assumed resistance up
lowers the upper edge \emph{and} raises the lower edge, so the interval shrinks from both sides
under one perturbation --- and it is verified numerically, not assumed.

The experimental surface, including what would falsify each claim, was fixed in a design
register before any code ran; divergences between the register and what happened are recorded
rather than edited into agreement. Zero-failure claims are reported with Clopper--Pearson 95\,\%
one-sided upper bounds \cite{clopper1934}; continuous outcomes with bootstrap confidence
intervals; paired comparisons with Wilcoxon signed-rank and effect sizes; monotonicity claims
with permutation Spearman.

\subsection{One vehicle, end to end}\label{sec:casestudy}

The table is a summary and summaries are easy to skim past, so here is a single vehicle followed
from arrival to dispatch, with every number drawn from the experiments above.

A compact urban robotaxi, 96S45P, arrives at a 350\,kW charger at 12\,\% charge. It has been on
the road since dawn; its pack is at whatever temperature the day has left it at, its cells have
aged unevenly, and nothing on board can measure the three quantities that decide how hard it may
be charged. Today it is the twelfth session of the day --- a private car accumulates this much
fast-charge stress in \gWeeks{} weeks.

\textbf{What it does today.} It applies the de-rate its manufacturer chose. That rate was picked
on evidence available before the vehicle shipped: a mild climate, cells not yet aged, the fastest
constant current that was safe on that population --- \bThreeTuned C. Deployed across the fleet
this vehicle actually belongs to, from $-10$ to 40\,\degC{} and out to end of life, that rate
violates a certified constraint in \textbf{\bThreeTunedViolPct\,\% of sessions}. To be safe
everywhere it must fall to \bThreeRequired C, and at that rate the vehicle reaches a 60\,\%
charge inside a \gtWindow-minute window in \textbf{\gtReachSixtyCc\,\% of sessions}. It mostly
does not go back into service; it sits.

\textbf{What it does with the certificate.} At each 30\,s step the filter evaluates the cell
model \evalCharge{} times --- always \evalCharge, never more --- and returns the largest current
that keeps voltage, temperature and the plating potential inside their limits for the next step,
at the worst corner of the envelope its estimator is allowed to assume. It identifies nothing. It
converges to nothing. Across \bThreeTrials{} deployment sessions it violates a certified
constraint \textbf{\bThreeZgViol{} times}, and it returns the vehicle to service at 60\,\% charge
in \textbf{\gtReachSixtyZg\,\% of sessions} --- \gtReachSixtyRatio$\times$ as many.

\textbf{What it costs, and where.} Not nothing, and not uniformly. On a mild day at 15\,\degC{} the
certificate delivers $+$\gtAmbFifteen{} points more charge than the fixed rate; on a 40\,\degC{}
afternoon it delivers \gtAmbForty{} points --- \emph{less} than the fixed rate, not more ---
because its own thermal margin puts its effective ceiling at \gtCeiling\,\degC{} and above that
ambient the cell can never sit below it, so the filter refuses. It is right to refuse --- it breached nothing in
\gtViol{} of \gtTrials{} sessions --- and refusing has a price. A fleet mean of
$+$\gtMean{} points is the average of those two regimes and is not a fact about any vehicle.

\textbf{And it runs on the hardware already in the car.} \fwText\,B of code and constants,
\fwRam\,B of RAM, no heap and no recursion, worst case \fwUsMax\,$\mu$s --- \fwSlotPct\,\% of a
10\,ms ISO~26262 task slot. It is a firmware change, not a new part, which is the difference
between a result and a deployment.

Over five years of that duty cycle, never recalibrated, the certificate breached
\gRoboViol{} of \gRoboSessions{} sessions.

\subsection{Where the method belongs, decided in advance}\label{sec:cross}

One cross-domain result decides which vehicles this is for, and it was fixed before the domain
experiments ran. The certificate's reserve is the width of the admissible interval, and it
predicts remaining endurance only where mission closure is \emph{envelope-driven} --- where the
constraint set shuts before the energy runs out. Regressing width against realised endurance:
$\rho = \xRhoAir$ for a delivery quadrotor and $+0.88$ for an eVTOL, both entirely
envelope-driven; $\rho = \xRhoGeo$ in GEO, partly; and \emph{negative} under ice, where closure is
energy-driven and the width says nothing.

That is a test an integrator can run on their own vehicle before adopting anything, and it is why
Table~\ref{tab:bydomain} has two columns where the incumbent is not beaten. Air and ground are
envelope-driven and the method helps there; water and deep space are not, and it does not.


\section{The same standard in every domain}\label{sec:transfer}

Two things were wrong with the per-domain comparisons, and both were ours.

\textbf{The standard was applied unevenly.} \S\ref{sec:transfer} caught a methodological error ---
a fixed de-rate tuned and evaluated on the same population --- and corrected it for the
\emph{ground} domain by tuning on a fleet a manufacturer could characterise in advance and
deploying on the fleet a vehicle actually meets. \S\ref{sec:transfer} then compared
against the air, water and space incumbents \emph{without} that correction: every fixed
state-of-charge threshold there was swept against the very population it was scored on. The
ground baseline was held to a stricter standard than the other three, which is backwards, and
water and GEO --- where the fixed rule won --- are precisely the results the correction would
move.

\textbf{And the ground metric was the wrong quantity.} \S\ref{sec:transfer} reports mean state of
charge after a fixed horizon. No operator buys state of charge. A robotaxi that is charging is a
robotaxi not earning, so the quantity is minutes until it can be dispatched again.

\subsection{Tune where you can characterise; deploy where the vehicle goes}

Each domain's incumbent threshold is chosen on a benign population --- mild ambient, cells not
yet aged --- taking the most permissive threshold that still recovers at the stated rate. It is
then deployed, unchanged, on the population the vehicle meets. The certificate reserve is put
through the identical procedure and, as everywhere else, is re-tuned not at all because it has
nothing to tune. Targets differ by domain and are stated, because in the aerial case no rule of
\emph{either} family recovers in 95\,\% of episodes and fixing the target there yields no
comparison rather than a result.

\subsection{Minutes back on the road}

Scored on dispatch time instead of state of charge, over \gtTrials{} sessions across the
deployment fleet against the CC--CV rate \S\ref{sec:transfer} showed is fleet-safe, the ground
picture separates into two facts the average was hiding.

The first is that the advantage is much larger than a mean SOC suggests. Within the
\gtWindow-minute window, \gtReachSixtyZg\,\% of sessions reach a 60\,\% charge under the certificate
against \gtReachSixtyCc\,\% under the fixed rate --- \gtReachSixtyRatio$\times$ as many vehicles
returned to service --- and at 70\,\% the fixed rate reaches it in \gtReachSeventyCc\,\% of sessions.
A fleet is sized on how many vehicles come back, not on the mean charge of those that do.

The second is that the fleet mean of $+$\gtMean{} points is an average taken across a change of
sign, and is therefore not a fact about any vehicle. By ambient the certificate delivers
$+$\gtAmbFifteen{} points at 15\,\degC{} and \gtAmbForty{} at 40\,\degC, crossing over between
\gtCrossLo{} and \gtCrossHi\,\degC.

The reversal is not the theorem failing, and it is not a mystery. The thermal margin puts the
certificate's effective ceiling at \gtCeiling\,\degC; above that ambient the cell can never sit
below it, so the filter refuses to charge. It is right to refuse --- it breached nothing in
\gtViol{} of \gtTrials{} sessions --- and refusing has a price. This is the passive-charging
ceiling of \S\ref{sec:domains} reappearing as a performance number rather than a safety one, and
it is the same margin-sizing question the extended version answers for pulses and does not
answer here, because a charging session really does last half an hour.


\section{Against the CBF quadratic program}\label{sec:cbf}

\S\ref{sec:related} places this work among predictive safety filters and control barrier
functions, and \S\ref{sec:transfer} then benchmarks it against CC--CV, a de-rate sweep and MPC.
That leaves the obvious comparison unmeasured. The CBF quadratic program is \emph{the} standard
safety filter, and this method claims to replace the optimisation inside it, so the comparison
is not optional.

The discrete-time CBF condition \cite{agrawal2017dcbf} asks, for each constraint written
$h(x) \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}
h\bigl(x_{k+1}(u)\bigr) \;\ge\; (1-\gamma)\,h(x_k), \qquad \gamma \in (0,1],
\end{equation}
and the filter projects the request onto the resulting set. For a cell model $h(x_{k+1})$ has no
closed form, so in practice the constraint is \textbf{linearised} in the input,
$h(x_k) + (\partial h/\partial u)\,u \ge (1-\gamma) h(x_k)$, which makes the problem a QP. That
linearisation is the entire difference between the two methods, and everything below is arranged
to isolate it. Both filters are given the identical constraint set, margins folded in the same
way, on identical plants and seeds.

\paragraph{The exact condition is this method.} Take the CBF condition and enforce it on the
\emph{true} model instead of a linearisation. Because each $h$ is monotone in $u$
(\S\ref{sec:cell}), $\{u : h(x_{k+1}(u)) \ge (1-\gamma)h(x_k)\}$ is an interval; $\gamma$ enters
as an additive shift of the margin, and bisection finds the edges. Measured over
\cExactStates{} states with $\gamma$ drawn from the sweep, the admissible set was a single
interval in \emph{all} of them (\cExactDisc{} disconnected), and the bisection edges agree with a
dense scan to \cExactDev\,A against a scan step of \cExactScan\,A.

So the contribution is not a rival to CBF. It is a statement of \emph{when the CBF-QP can be
solved exactly at fixed cost} --- and for monotone constraints in a scalar input, the answer is
always. That is a stronger position than beating the QP, and it is the one the geometry actually
supports.


\paragraph{What this does not claim.} The QP is cheaper --- \cQpEval{} evaluations against
\cZgEval, and for a scalar input its solution is a clip rather than an iterative solve --- so
nothing here is a speed claim. The claim is that at equal safety the exact filter delivers more
charge, and that exactness is available at a fixed, known cost.




\subsection{The firmware, compiled}\label{sec:firmware}

Everything above is an estimate. Bytes of state were counted from a Python dictionary, table
sizes from an interpolation error study, and ``needs no FPU'' from the observation that a
midpoint is an integer operation. Those are reasonable ways to size a footprint and no way at
all to establish one: \emph{fits in a kilobyte} is a claim about a binary, and there was no
binary.

\texttt{firmware/zeroguard.c} is one. The charge-direction certificate in C99, with the integer
bisection and a float32 model, no heap, no recursion, no variable-length structure, and every
loop bound a compile-time constant --- so worst-case execution time is a property of the source
rather than of the data, which is the property a safety task slot is actually sized against. Its
constants are \emph{generated} from the same calibration files the Python filter loads; had they
been transcribed, the two could drift and every agreement number below would be measuring a copy
of itself.

Built at \texttt{-Os} with \texttt{-Wall -Wextra -Werror} and no warnings, it occupies
\textbf{\fwText\,B} of code and constants --- \fwOcv{} of that the open-circuit-voltage table ---
with \fwData\,B of writable data and \fwRam\,B of RAM live across a call. One call is exactly
\fwEvals{} model evaluations.

\paragraph{And how long it takes.} The footprint became a measurement above; the timing had not.
The \worstLatency\,$\mu$s quoted in \S\ref{sec:cross} is the \emph{reference} implementation
timed in Python, which tells a reader nothing about a microcontroller. Timed on the compiled
routine over \fwBenchStates{} states at 2\,000 repetitions each, a call takes \fwUsMed\,$\mu$s at
the median and \fwUsMax\,$\mu$s at worst --- \fwSlotPct\,\% of a 10\,ms ISO~26262 slot. The
\fwSpread$\times$ spread between worst and median is not data dependence within a path: it is the
three paths \texttt{zg\_limit} has --- infeasible at the anchor (one evaluation), feasible at the
ceiling (two), or the full bisection (\fwEvals) --- and the worst case is the longest of three
fixed-length routines, which is what lets a task slot be sized at all.

\paragraph{Does it agree with the reference?} Not bit-for-bit, and it was never going to: the
reference computes in float64 and bisects in floating point, the firmware computes in float32
and bisects in integers. The question is which way the disagreement points. Over \fwStates{}
states with an identical pack and identical margins, the two never disagreed about
\emph{feasibility} (\fwMismatch{} mismatches), and the returned current differed by at most
\fwMaxDev\,mA against a bisection quantum of \fwQuantum\,mA --- the firmware exceeded the
reference by more than one quantum in \fwAbove{} states. Where it was higher at all, the extra
current is worth \fwVcost\,mV of terminal voltage (\fwVfrac\,\% of the voltage margin) and
\fwTcost\,K. The guarantee survives the port with the margin it already carries, and does not
need a new one.


\section{Against constrained reinforcement learning}\label{sec:crl}

\S\ref{sec:related} says constrained RL offers expectation-level guarantees, ``which are the
wrong shape for a constraint that must hold on every step of every episode'', cites
\cite{achiam2017cpo,altman1999constrained}, and then benchmarks against CC--CV, a de-rate sweep,
MPC and the CBF quadratic program --- never against the thing it dismisses. In a paper whose
method is to measure rather than assert, that was the one assertion left standing.

The baseline is a Lagrangian-constrained policy trained the way the cited literature trains one:
charge delivered minus a multiplier times \emph{expected} constraint cost, with the multiplier
raised while the measured violation rate sits above a budget and lowered when it falls below
\cite{stooke2020pid}. It is given a richer state feedback than the certificate uses, the same
cell, the same envelope, and --- like every other baseline in \S\ref{sec:transfer} --- it is
fitted on a characterisation fleet and deployed on the one the vehicle meets.

\paragraph{It meets its budget, and that is the problem.} Trained down to the tightest budget the
policy violates in \rlBestTrain\,\% of characterisation episodes. Deployed, it violates in
\rlBestDeploy\,\% of sessions; no budget in the sweep reaches zero, and the worst reaches
\rlWorst\,\%. The certificate on the same fleet: \rlZgViol{} of \rlTrials, CP95 \rlZgCP\,\%.

\paragraph{``You did not train it enough'' is the obvious objection, and it does not hold.} It is
a fair objection --- with a small batch a 1\,\% violation rate is not even measurable, so the
search cannot see the budget it is chasing. Retrained at the tightest budget with
\rlHardIters{} iterations and a batch of \rlHardBatch, the policy reaches \rlHardTrain\,\% in
training and still violates in \rlHardDeploy\,\% of deployment sessions --- a gap of
\rlGapPoints{} points. More search moves the deployment rate by a few points and does not
approach zero.

That gap is the entire claim, stated precisely. Constrained RL is not bad at this problem; it is
optimising a different functional. A bound on the \emph{expectation} of violations over a
training distribution says nothing about any particular episode drawn from a different one, and
a vehicle experiences particular episodes. No amount of compute converts an expectation into a
per-step guarantee, because they are not the same object.





\section{It is not about batteries}\label{sec:motor}

The Lean development proves the certificate sound for an \emph{arbitrary} predicate over an
arbitrary type carrying a $\le$ relation. Nothing in it mentions electrochemistry. And every
platform in this paper is a lithium cell, so the generality has been proved and never
demonstrated --- which is precisely the gap a reader should be suspicious of, because a proof
of generality that is never exercised is indistinguishable from a proof that is vacuous in
practice.

So the identical filter --- \texttt{zeroguard/anchored.py}, not a line changed, not an adapter
written --- is run on a \mtPower\,kW permanent-magnet traction motor. The physics shares nothing
with a cell: no state of charge, no plating, no open-circuit voltage, no diffusion. What it
shares is the only thing the theorem asks for.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{The input} is phase current amplitude, a scalar.
\item \textbf{Winding temperature} rises with $I^2R$ copper loss --- monotone for the same
reason a cell's does, which is the one piece of physics the two plants have in common.
\item \textbf{Terminal voltage} is $\sqrt{(k_e\omega + IR)^2 + (\omega L I)^2}$ against the DC
link --- monotone in $I$, by a completely different mechanism than a cell's overpotential.
\item \textbf{Demagnetisation} is a current ceiling that \emph{tightens as the rotor heats} ---
structurally the same shape as the plating cap, from unrelated physics. It is written $\ge$ and
bounds current from above, so its \emph{side} must be declared rather than inferred, exactly as
\S\ref{sec:theory} requires.
\item \textbf{And a floor.} A vehicle holding a grade must deliver $\tau\omega \ge P_{\mathrm{dem}}$
or it rolls backwards. Zero current is safe for every cap and serves nothing --- which is the
Anchored Collapse case, on a plant that is not a battery.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{It holds, and it holds for the stated reason.} Every channel is monotone in current
across \mtStates{} sampled states, with the worst violation exactly zero. The admissible set was
a single interval in \emph{all} \mtScan{} states scanned (\mtDisc{} disconnected), and the
bisection's edges agree with a dense scan to \mtDev\,A against a scan step of \mtStep\,A.
Climbing a steepening grade until the envelope closes, the filter breached nothing in
\mtRuns{} runs (CP95 \mtCP\,\%); the envelope closed in \mtClosed{} of them and the reserve
warned first in \mtWarned, with a median lead of \mtLead\,s and a fifth percentile of
\mtLeadP\,s.

The cost took the three values the theory predicts and no others: \mtPaths{} evaluations ---
caps only, caps plus a floor check, or both bisections --- with a maximum of \mtEvalsMax, which
is exactly the discharge bound of \S\ref{sec:theory}. It was not tuned to match; it is the same
code counting the same probes on a different plant.

\paragraph{What this changes.} The contribution is not a battery result that happens to
generalise. It is a statement about a class of control problems --- \emph{scalar input, monotone
constraints, a known-safe anchor} --- of which fast charging is one instance and a traction
motor is another. The battery is where the problem was found, not where the method ends. We
claim only what is demonstrated: two instances, one of them not a cell, certified by the same
code at the same proved cost.


\section{External validation: the assumptions against measured cells}\label{sec:external}

Everything to this point is simulation. The filter is checked against a reduced-order model,
and that model was calibrated against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman simulation. That is a defensible
chain and it is still a chain of models, so the fair question is what happens when the
assumptions meet a real cell.

This section answers it with the NASA Ames Prognostics Center of Excellence battery data set:
\nCells{} 18650 cells cycled to failure with periodic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy,
published by a different laboratory, years before this work, for a different purpose. Nothing
here was produced by us.

The design point is that \textbf{the load-bearing assumptions are testable without any model at
all.} The method does not need the ROM to be right about NASA's cells. It needs three things to
be true of real cells, and each is a chemistry-independent statement about the shape of
degradation.

\subsection{The resistance bound holds}

The filter never identifies resistance; it assumes a \emph{bound} on it, taken from a datasheet
before the vehicle ships. If real cells leave that bound, every certificate in this paper is
void. Across \nEIS{} impedance measurements, \textbf{\nInsideFrac\,\% lie inside the assumed
bound $s_R = \nBound$} (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}a). Restricted to the life the certificate
actually claims --- cells at or above 80\,\% of initial capacity, the standard automotive end of
life --- the figure is \textbf{\nInsideEolFrac\,\% of \nEolMeas{} measurements}.

Over the full measured life, including cells NASA ran down to \nCapWorst{} of initial capacity,
\nViolations{} measurement exceeds the bound, on cell \nWorstCell{}, by 0.1\,\%. That is
reported rather than trimmed: the bound is tight, and it is not a comfortable over-estimate.

\subsection{Heat generation is monotone in current, measured}

Hypothesis (A2) is the other load-bearing assumption, and it too can be checked without a model
--- though the obvious way of doing it does not work, and why is worth recording. Correlating
instantaneous $\mathrm{d}T/\mathrm{d}t$ against instantaneous current returns nothing usable:
stratifying by $T-T_{\mathrm{amb}}$ to control for cooling gives rank correlations that
\emph{flip sign} between strata ($-0.34$ in the coldest, $+0.54$ in the warmest). NASA measures
surface temperature, the core leads it by minutes, and over a charge whose total rise is under
3\,K the thermal lag dominates the signal. Reporting either sign from that test would be an
artefact of the instrument.

The lag-free formulation is an energy balance over a whole cycle, which does not care how the
heat arrives: total ohmic generation goes as $\int I^2\,\mathrm{d}t$, so a cycle's peak
temperature rise should increase with it. Across \nDisCycles{} measured discharge cycles, with
currents to \nMaxI\,A and temperature rises to \nMaxDT\,K, the rank correlation is
\textbf{$\rho = \nDisRho$} ($p < 0.0005$; Fig.~\ref{fig:external}b). The charge cycles give
$\nChgRho$, which is weaker for a mundane reason: NASA charges almost everything at a constant
1.5\,A, so there is little current variation to correlate against.

\subsection{Where the model does not transfer, and whether that matters}

The harder question is how the ROM itself behaves on a cell it was never fitted to --- a 5\,Ah
NMC model applied to $\sim$2\,Ah LCO cells with capacity rescaled and nothing else refitted.
Measured one step ahead the question is meaningless, because NASA samples every $\sim$9\,s over
which the temperature moves about 0.01\,K and predicting ``unchanged'' scores well. Integrated
forward over a \nHorizon\,s horizon under the measured current, the transferred model scores
RMSE \nRMSE\,\degC{} against a persistence baseline of \nPersist\,\degC{} --- a skill of
\nSkill{}.

The safety-relevant question is not the magnitude but the \emph{sign}. A model that
over-predicts temperature yields a conservative certificate; one that under-predicts yields an
unsafe one. \textbf{This transfer is not conservative}: the bias is \nBias\,\degC{} and it
over-predicts in only \nOverFrac\,\% of forecasts. What rescues it is the margin. The worst
under-prediction at the 5th percentile is \nUnderWorst\,\degC{} against a thermal margin of
12.5\,\degC{} --- a factor of \textbf{\nMarginCover} of cover (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}c). The
margin was not designed for cross-chemistry transfer; it happens to absorb it, and that is a
fact about this data rather than a guarantee.

\subsection{The filter permits a real protocol, and refuses where it said it would}

A safety filter that refuses everything is safe and useless. Given the end-of-life resistance
bound and the full throttled margin --- its most conservative configuration --- the certificate
permits \textbf{\nPermitFrac\,\%} of NASA's measured charge steps, with a median headroom of
\nHeadroom$\times$ the applied current. Of the refusals, \nRefuseCeil\,\% are cells measured
above the throttled ceiling of \nCeiling\,\degC{}: the same frontier \S\ref{sec:domains}
predicts from the margin alone, appearing unprompted in someone else's measurements.

\subsection{One test that did not work}

It was tempting to go further and ask whether the cells the filter would have refused are the
cells that actually degraded. The mechanism would have been clean --- NASA cycled some cells at
4\,\degC{}, the plating cap falls to a 0.70C floor below about 5.5\,\degC{}, and plating is what
degrades a cold-charged cell.

The association is not stable. Its sign \emph{changes} with how the capacity baseline is
defined --- first measured discharge against the median of the largest few --- because NASA's
first discharge on \nBadBaselines{} of \nCapCells{} cells is partial, giving baselines as low as
0.068\,Ah for a 2\,Ah cell. Across the four defensible combinations of baseline and fade metric,
$\rho$ ranges from $\nAssocLo$ to $\nAssocHi$, with significant results of both signs, and
ambient temperature and cycle count sit behind both quantities. All four are reported in
Table-form in the results file rather than one. \textbf{A result that flips sign with a
defensible change of definition is not a result}, and selecting the flattering version would be
the most misleading thing this study could do.

\section{Against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman plant}\label{sec:dfn}

Every vehicle number in \S\ref{sec:domains} comes from a plant we wrote. The
reduced-order model is defensible and it is still ours, so the question a battery reader asks is
not whether this was tested on hardware but the sharper one underneath: \emph{how do you know the
ROM is right?}

DFN \cite{doyle1993,newman2004,fuller1994} is the field's reference model --- porous-electrode
transport, solid-phase diffusion, Butler--Volmer kinetics, parameterised and validated against
cells by others --- and it is a \emph{different model class}, not the same equations with
different numbers. That is what makes it worth running. The envelope of \S\ref{sec:method} varies
resistance, capacity and plating scale \emph{within} the ROM's structure; DFN inflicts
misspecification the envelope was never written to cover: concentration gradients the ROM has no
state for, kinetics it approximates, and a plating potential that is an actual electrode quantity
rather than an affine proxy. The filter sees only measured SOC and temperature, propagating its
own relaxation state between them, because that is all a battery management system has.

\paragraph{What this is and is not.} The ROM's coefficients were fitted to the LG M50, and the
PyBaMM parameter set used here (\dSet) describes that cell, so this is \textbf{not} an
independent-cell test and must not be read as one. It is a model-class test: same cell, higher
fidelity. \S\ref{sec:external} covers the other axis --- \nCells{} cells from another laboratory
that nothing was ever fitted to. Neither substitutes for the other; together they bracket the two
ways a model can be wrong.


\paragraph{Closed loop.} With the certificate computed on the ROM and the plant a full DFN cell,
across \dSessions{} charging sessions: \dViolV{} voltage breaches, \dViolT{} temperature
breaches. DFN's own peak values were \dPeakV\,V against \dVmaxLim{} and \dPeakT\,\degC{} against
\dTmaxLim, so the cell was worked, not idled.

The plating result is the one worth pausing on. The ROM enforces an affine proxy; DFN reports the
negative electrode surface potential difference, whose crossing of zero \emph{is} the
thermodynamic onset of lithium deposition. In \dPlateClean{} of \dSessions{} sessions the real
electrode potential stayed above onset, with a minimum of $+$\dMinPhi\,mV. The proxy the paper has
enforced throughout, and has been careful never to certify, does the physical job it was built
for --- measured against electrode physics rather than against itself.


\subsection{And the floor family, which is what is new}\label{sec:dfndischarge}

Everything above is the charge direction, which is the published case. Anchored Collapse's
contribution is the \emph{floor}: a vehicle serving a load has a constraint bounding its current
from below, and a second bisection finds that edge. So the half of the theorem that was already
known had external physics behind it and the half that is new did not.

A DFN cell serves a constant electrical load, from \dcLoadLo{} to \dcLoadHi\,W per cell, while
the anchored filter chooses the current. Four constraints are live and they pull both ways:
temperature and state of charge cap the current, terminal voltage caps it too (a cell sags under
load), and the load power is a floor.

Across \dcSteps{} filtered discharge steps the DFN reports \dcBreachV{} voltage and \dcBreachT{}
temperature breaches (CP95 \dcCP\,\%). Wherever the certificate reported the load meetable, the
DFN delivered it --- never below \dcServed\,\% of demand. Above 100\,\% is not an error:
\texttt{Platform.anchor} sizes current at the lowest bus voltage the constraints permit, so it
deliberately over-estimates and the cell then sits above that voltage.

\paragraph{And the refusals were audited.} The interesting case for a floor is not the step it
permits but the step it refuses. The envelope closed on \dcRefused{} of \dcSteps{} steps, meaning
the load could not be met inside the caps. Pushing to meet it anyway and asking the DFN what
would have happened, \dcRight{} of those refusals were correct and \dcWrong{} were not ---
\dcPrecision\,\%. The remainder are steps where there was room and the margin took it, which is
the price of a margin and is reported as such rather than folded into the success count.


\section{Cycle life: a claim that did not survive}\label{sec:cyclelife}

Plating is the dominant fast-charge degradation mechanism, so a filter that prevents plating
ought to show up as capacity retained. That inference is tempting, it is the largest claim
available to this work, and \textbf{it is not supported}.

\paragraph{Not with our own aging model.} The ROM ships one, and using it would have been the
cheap route and a worthless one: its plating term accumulates damage proportional to
$\mathrm{clip}(\text{margin} - \phi, 0, \infty)\cdot|I|$, where the margin is \emph{the same
threshold the certificate enforces}. A filter holding $\phi \ge$ margin by construction scores
exactly zero damage by construction. So the plant is DFN with the full degradation stack ---
irreversible plating, solvent-diffusion-limited SEI, porosity change --- and the numbers are
DFN's own capacity-loss variables, which do not know the certificate exists.

\paragraph{The result.} Cycling \clCycles{} times at \clAmb\,\degC{} between 15\,\% and 70\,\%
with a 1C discharge, capacity lost is \clCertFade\,\% under the certificate, \clSlowFade\,\%
under CC--CV 0.5C and \clFastFade\,\% under CC--CV 1.5C. The spread is \clSpread{} points. The
certificate is \emph{marginally worse} than the slow de-rate (\clVsSafe{} points), and its
advantage over the fast rate, \clVsFast{} points, is noise.

\paragraph{Why, and what it does not overturn.} The mechanism is the one the extended version
already identified and it applies with more force here. DFN's plating variable is dominated by a
slow background side reaction that accrues with cold time and total throughput --- which every
controller spends similarly, since all three deliver the same charge --- rather than by the
onset crossings the certificate prevents. Those crossings are real: the fast rate crossed in
\clFastOnset{} of \clCycles{} cycles and the certificate in \clCertOnset. They are simply not
what drives capacity fade in this regime.

So the extended version stands and no life claim follows from it. The honest scope of the safety
argument is the deposition onset --- the first link of the chain, where metallic lithium is laid
down rather than consumed in a film --- and not calendar or cycle life, which this experiment
looked for and did not find.


\section{Deployability: what it costs on a BMS microcontroller}\label{sec:embedded}

The claim that the certificate ``fits inside any microcontroller a battery management system
already contains'' was an assertion about software that had never been compiled for one. It
should be measured or withdrawn.

\paragraph{It holds no growing state.} Over \embCalls{} projections the heap grows by
\embBytesPerCall{} bytes per call --- Python's own object churn. There is no list, no queue, no
history: nothing a C implementation would need to allocate.

\paragraph{Single precision is adequate but not one-sided.} A Cortex-M4F has a
single-precision FPU and no double. Across \embSpStates{} states the single-precision filter
deviates from double by at most \embSpWorstmA\,mA, which is \embSpFrac\,\% of full scale ---
but it deviates in \emph{both} directions, permitting slightly more current than double in
\embSpLooseCount{} of them. Negligible in magnitude against a 12.5\,K thermal margin; not a
guarantee, and not claimed as one.

\paragraph{The search needs no floating point at all, and there the direction \emph{is}
provable.} Representing current as an integer count of quanta makes the bisection's only
arithmetic \texttt{mid = (lo + hi) >> 1}, exact on any integer unit. Rounding the midpoint down
makes the result conservative by construction: across \embIntStates{} states at
\embIntBits{}-bit resolution (\embIntQuantum\,A quanta) the integer search returned a value
above the real-valued answer \textbf{\embIntAbove{} times}. For a build that needs a provable
direction of error, the FPU-free one is the one to ship.

\paragraph{Footprint.} \textbf{\embRam{} bytes of RAM} --- live state plus \embConstants{}
calibration constants --- and \textbf{\embFlash{} bytes} of lookup tables for an FPU-free build
(\embLutExp{} entries for the Arrhenius exponential, \embLutAsinh{} for the inverse hyperbolic
sine, 128 for open-circuit voltage, each sized for 0.1\,\% interpolation error). Constant stack,
no recursion, no variable-length structure, \evalCharge{}--\evalDischarge{} model evaluations.
That is smaller than the interrupt vector table on the parts in question.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

Stated in full, because several of them are load-bearing.

\paragraph{Most results are model-versus-model, and two are not.} The vehicle results in
\S\ref{sec:domains} are reduced-order-model level. Two experiments are not:
\S\ref{sec:dfn} runs the certificate against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman plant and \S\ref{sec:cyclelife}
cycles one, both with PyBaMM's own degradation physics. Those are model-class transfer tests on
the cell the ROM was fitted to, not independent-cell tests --- \S\ref{sec:external} covers that
axis separately, and validates the \emph{assumptions} against measured cells rather than any
vehicle result. Neither substitutes for hardware.

\paragraph{No lifetime benefit was found.} Preventing plating ought to buy cycle life, and
\S\ref{sec:cyclelife} looked for that and did not find it: over \clCycles{} cold cycles the three
controllers lose within \clSpread{} points of the same capacity. The safety argument is scoped to
the deposition onset and no life or warranty claim follows from it. That is a limitation of the
result, not of the experiment --- the experiment is the reason the claim is not in the paper.

\paragraph{The certified set is $\{T, V\}$; plating is enforced, never certified.}
\S\ref{sec:cell} gives the reason and \S\ref{sec:domains} shows it is not a technicality: in a
sealed hull at any water temperature, the binding constraint is the one the theorem does not
certify. Extending to plating needs a control-invariance argument the $u=0$ backup cannot
supply, and we do not have one.

\paragraph{One cell, sixteen vehicles.} Every platform uses the coefficients of
\S\ref{sec:cell}, because that is the cell the model was fitted to. Where a class would really
use a different cell --- the rotorcraft --- the substitution is explicit ($0.40\times$
resistance, $0.60\times$ capacity), is an extrapolation beyond the calibration, and every aerial
number carries it.



\paragraph{The vehicle results remain simulation.} \S\ref{sec:external} tests the assumptions
against measured cells, but every \emph{vehicle} number in \S\ref{sec:domains}
comes from a plant we wrote, and a filter tested against models chosen to satisfy its hypotheses
is weaker than one tested on hardware. Three things partially answer it: three of the four media
\emph{break} the original hypothesis, one platform is refused outright, and fourteen of
\vExperiments{} experiments contradicted the hypothesis they were written to test --- the
register was not built so that everything passes. \S\ref{sec:dfn} answers the sharpest form of the objection without a bench: the certificate is
run against a Doyle--Fuller--Newman plant, a different model class, and holds on both certified
channels and on DFN's own electrode plating potential. That is not hardware and does not claim to
be --- DFN is validated against cells by others, so what \S\ref{sec:dfn} buys is one link of that
chain, not the whole of it. Running the filter in the loop on real cells remains the natural next
step and is not claimed here.

\section{Conclusion}

The safety guarantee an autonomous vehicle's battery needs turns out to require neither an
optimiser nor an identified model, only a geometric fact: when constraints are monotone in a
scalar input and one input is known safe, the admissible set is an interval, and an interval can
be found by bisection at fixed cost.

Getting that from a cell to a vehicle required noticing that the theorem's apparent failure in
flight was a misreading. Zero current is still safe for every temperature and voltage constraint
on a discharging pack; what zero cannot do is serve the load the vehicle exists to serve, and
that is a floor rather than a cap. Anchored Collapse adds the floor family and a second
bisection, and nothing else --- \evalDischarge{} fixed evaluations against the original
\evalCharge{}.

Across \vPlatforms{} platforms in four media and \vExperiments{} experiments totalling
\vTotalEpisodes{} episodes, nothing was breached while the certificate reported feasible, the
admissible set was a single interval in all \scanTotal{} dense scans, and the worst latency
anywhere was \worstLatency\,$\mu$s.

The results we would most want a reader to take are the ones that did not come out as intended.
A certificate that refuses a cell chemistry for a mission class before takeoff is doing
something a runtime guard is not normally asked to do. A domain in which the binding constraint
is the one the theorem explicitly does not certify is a limitation a less careful register would
not have surfaced. And a warning that leaves \uIceClosureMed{} minutes when the vehicle
needs \uIceTransit{} is a negative result whose forced abort rule --- act on a threshold in the
reserve, not on its exhaustion --- is more useful than the positive result would have been.
